# Exact flat solver and checks

Run with a standard Python 3 interpreter and assertions enabled.

```sh
cd notes/affine-elimination
python experiments.py
```

The command rebuilds `experiments.json`, including the raw small inputs, seeds, variable order, every projected state's dense size and width, and counters. The code's decision entry point is `flat_elimination.decide(raw_flats, n)`. To inspect a chosen order, call `normalize` once, then `project` with the next zero-based variable ID. The order used for the small-instance traces is ascending. The quadratic-family experiment has its own driver and outputs.

## Representation and simplification

A stored row `r` means that the dot product of `r >> 1` with the assignment, over F₂, equals `r & 1`. A tuple of rows describes their common solution flat. The solver accepts points outside the union of all listed flats. The empty family accepts every point and the empty tuple as a member excludes every point. `clause` maps a DIMACS clause to its falsifying rows, including negative literals and empty clauses. `parity(ids, rhs)` excludes the hyperplane on which the required XOR equation fails. Thus each ordinary CNF literal uses one packed row. With variable names renumbered consecutively, this dense encoding of a CNF of bit length N uses O(N²) bits. To start from a formula tree, one may first encode binary gates by Tseitin clauses, enforce the output and existentially quantify gate values. This adds linearly many clauses and occurrences in the size of the tree. The driver takes the resulting constraints as input.

Each consistent flat has a unique reduced row echelon form in descending pivot order. Every operation preserves the covered set. An inconsistent row system contributes the empty set. An inner flat contributes nothing if its rows imply all equations of a larger stored flat. Containment is checked by row reduction of every outer equation against the inner basis, with its RHS included. No general logical-equivalence test is used.

Two distinct flats with the same linear parts in RREF are parallel cosets with equations Ax=s and Ax=t. Choose j where s and t differ. For coordinates i other than j, retain row i where the RHS agrees and XOR row j into row i where the RHS differs. This defines the coset preimage of the two-point affine line {s,t}: its kernel in syndrome space is generated by s⊕t. The merged system therefore covers exactly the original pair. The implementation pairs parallel cosets in deterministic order, removes containment and repeats until a pass has no merge. Each merge decreases the number of flats. Its output can depend on pairing. The procedure neither promises a minimum cover nor detects coverage by every union of incomparable flats.

To eliminate z, split each incident flat using any row with z coefficient one. The cleared rows give a domain D and the pivot gives a forbidden value z=f(y). Carry every cylinder (a flat with no z coefficient). Two distinct graphs jointly forbid both choices exactly on D_i∩D_j∩{f_i⊕f_j=1}. Emit each such intersection as one row system and simplify. Single-valued graphs cannot themselves block both choices. This proves pointwise existential projection and extends to any number of z-containing literals in a clause. After every variable is eliminated the residual is either the empty family or the singleton family containing the whole 0-dimensional space, giving immediate decision. The entry point can also stop when either constant appears earlier.

An optional shortcut follows from the same proof. If one graph's domain contains every other graph's domain, checking pairs incident to that graph suffices. At any point where two other graphs disagree, the distinguished graph is defined and must disagree with one of them. A required unit affine equation involving z always provides such a full-domain graph. This is substitution by an affine pivot. The measured generic solver enumerates all graph pairs. The separate global Gaussian benchmark specializes the all-parity decision problem.

## Measured sizes and worst-case accounting

The dense bit length used in the traces counts Elias gamma codes for n+1 and m+1, followed for each flat by the gamma code of its row count plus one and n+1 bits per row. Column IDs stay fixed throughout elimination. A reduced flat has width equal to its codimension. The raw input's encoded length is measured before normalization. Algebraic coefficients are single bits. `max_packed_row_bits` additionally measures the actual integer bit length of a row. The reported dense bit length measures serialized data. Runtime counters include row XORs, pivot membership tests, graph intersections, containment queries and pair merges. Pre-simplification flat and row maxima, raw candidate widths and maximum RREF basis rows are also recorded. The named counts exclude interpreter, tuple, sorting and hash-table work.

Here is a conservative bound for the implementation in terms of its current size, including those latter operations. Let m be the number of normalized current flats, T=1+m+binom(m,2) and L=2+n+ceil(log₂(T+1)). One projection has at most T output candidates before simplification. A graph pair contributes at most 2n−1 raw rows to RREF, of at most n+1 bits each. Only one raw intersection is built at a time. Canonical flats have at most n rows. Coset factoring and containment never increase the number of flats in a pass.

A row reduction on O(n) rows has O(n²) pivot probes and row operations, plus pivot-key sorting, bookkeeping and dictionary access. Even charging a linear scan through all pivot keys at each dictionary access, and comparison of O(L)-bit keys, O(L⁵) bit operations suffice per RREF on a bit-cost random-access machine. Containment costs O(L³) per tested pair. Tuple comparisons or hashing one flat cost O(L²). Building a set or groups by direction costs O(T² L²) even allowing a linear number of probes or key comparisons per insert. Sorting the T flat keys costs O(T log(T+1) L²). These costs cover each antichain and grouping pass in O(T² L⁴), allowing for O(L)-bit indexing and counters. At most T passes and T additional RREF calls arise from merging. Graph construction, all candidate RREFs and carry rows fit the same bound O(T³ L⁶) bit operations per projection, with O(T L³) bits of working storage including index overhead. These are deliberately loose bounds. A sequential tape machine may scan the entire working storage for every indexed access, giving the still-polynomial-in-current-size bound O(T⁴ L¹⁰). Initial normalization on the input's total row count has the same polynomial character, processing each input row once against a basis of at most n rows before cover simplification. The trivial inputs with no rows are decided at once. Otherwise the dense input encoding already pays n+1 bits for a row. These bounds account for potentially quadratic output count in one step, and give no unrestricted polynomial bound on a run of n projections in the original input length.

For a pure system of m parity requirements, `pure_baselines.gaussian_decision` flips each forbidden RHS and performs independent global forward Gaussian elimination, deciding inconsistency by the row 0=1. There are at most n pivots, O(nm+n) coefficient tests and O(nm) packed row XORs. This takes O((m+1)(n+1)(n+log(m+2))) bit operations in a bit-cost random-access implementation. Pure 2-CNF uses a separate implication graph with two vertices per variable and at most two directed arcs per clause. Its two iterative DFS passes take O((n+m) log(n+m+2)) bit operations under the same address-cost convention after parsing. Decoding dense row masks into endpoints can add O(m(n+1)) bit operations. These baselines are decision procedures for their corresponding pure inputs.

## Reproducible outcomes

* All 16 assignments to the four expressions in the supplied identity and all eight assignments to the one-in-three reduction passed.
* Enumerating 697 zero- through three-row systems on three variables yielded exactly 51 distinct nonempty affine flats. The tests checked membership of every row system and uniqueness of the normalized representatives.
* For all 1,326 unordered pairs with repetition of these flats, the tests compared every output point with a truth-table projection, on each of the three variables, with both factoring settings. This is 7,956 projection checks. All 256 truth tables represented as unions of falsifying points contributed 1,536 more.
* Fourteen named small instances, each with factoring enabled and disabled, supplied 28 full traces and 140 states including their normalized inputs. For every state the surviving truth table was compared with pointwise existential quantification of an independently evaluated original. This includes satisfiable and unsatisfiable pure parity, 2-SAT, positive one-in-three, pigeonhole instances, constant formulas and mixed seeds 2718, 31415 and 65537.
* The independent Gaussian and SCC decision procedures passed 128 further truth-table comparisons on seeds 0 through 63. Two 64-variable chains impose x₀=0 and xᵢ=xᵢ₊₁. Requiring x₆₃=0 gives the all-zero witness. Requiring x₆₃=1 yields 0=1 on summing all equations. Global Gaussian and generic elimination agreed on both. Each chain used 4,446 input bits, 2,144 Gaussian coefficient probes and 64 Gaussian row XORs. The generic path emitted 63 graph-pair candidates in each case.

One minimal growth example is (x∨z)∧(y∨(z⊕t)). Eliminating z gives x∨y∨¬t. Its unique falsifying point (x,y,t)=(0,0,1) has codimension three. Every nonempty flat of codimension at most two in three variables contains at least two points, so even arbitrarily many affine clauses of width at most two cannot express this projection. Two incident flats are necessary to block both branches in the absence of cylinders, and three residual coordinates are necessary for codimension three. The trace `minimal_width_growth` checks the exact surviving row system under both simplification settings.
