# Primary sources and novelty record

The closest result is the exact minimum number of affine flats covering the odd inner-product set. Cohen and Shinkar define DNF of parities by the fan-in of its top OR gate. Its terms are exactly affine flats, including constants. Their Theorem 1.5 gives the optimum $2^k-1$ on $2k$ input bits. In §6 they bound the cardinality of any flat in the odd set by $2^{k-1}$. Fixing each nonzero left input gives a matching partition. Complementation turns their result directly into the minimum number of affine clauses for the even inner-product predicate. This holds with arbitrary sharing of parity computations at the bottom of that circuit. [Cohen and Shinkar, ECCC TR14-099, §1 and §6](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/099/download/)

The application here is to a specified projection of a small mixed SAT instance. Products have compact gate constraints before projection. Eliminating the gate variables leaves exactly the even inner-product predicate. Thus the lower bound applies to *any* flat-cover representation of that residual, including the best semantic deduplication, merging and choice of affine coordinates. It bounds the number of clauses with the stated output variables and grammar. A circuit allowing a parity of AND gates computes the odd predicate with $k$ ANDs and one parity, whose output can be complemented for the even predicate, and keeping auxiliary variables can encode that circuit succinctly. No priority claim is made for the circuit lower bound, gate encodings or their combination. The concrete order comparison and reproducible accounting are the purpose of this research cycle.

## Mixed testbed and elimination

- Schaefer's introductory relation $R(x,y,z)$ contains exactly the three triples of weight one. His SAT instance is a conjunction of applications to variables, so the hard source problem is positive one-in-three SAT. The example on p. 216 even permits a repeated variable. Each occurrence can be replaced by one odd-parity equation and three pairwise negative binary clauses. The parity condition accepts weights 1 and 3, the clauses accept weights 0 and 1, and the intersection has weight 1. This argument also covers repeated arguments. The translation makes a constant number of copies of the original variable identifiers, takes linear time and output length in an explicit occurrence encoding, and introduces no variables. Guessing an assignment and checking input rows takes polynomial time. Hence the mixed decision problem is NP-complete. The replacement and the membership proof here are deductions from the source theorem. [Schaefer, pp. 216–218, especially Theorem 2.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/max-sat/p216-schaefer.pdf)
- The ordinary-clause case is Davis and Putnam's Rule III on p. 210. Dechter's §2 recasts directional resolution as join followed by projection in a bucket, and analyzes the induced width of a supplied order. Our elimination uses the same two possible pivot values, with a clause's forbidden assignments given by simultaneous linear equations. Gaussian elimination then handles branch restriction and intersection. For the separate rank-parameterized implementation, the table key consists of values of a row basis of suffix forms. Its parameter measures how many independent forms cross each prefix in the specified procedure. The sources establish the general elimination framework. We have not identified a priority result for this particular choice of table coordinates. [Davis and Putnam, Rule III](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs357/DP60.pdf) · [Dechter, §2](https://ics.uci.edu/~dechter/publications/r48b.pdf)
- Linear-code trellises provide another precedent for indexing an intermediate state by syndrome data. Kiely et al. study state dimensions of minimal trellises for a linear block code. The rank in our algorithm is computed from coefficient rows of original factors that have already been touched, restricted to the suffix, for a supplied order. It is a sufficient table dimension for mixed clauses. No minimal-trellis claim or comparison theorem is required. [Kiely et al., author archive](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/b1pat-qd236)
- Disjunctions of parity equations are established as *linear clauses* in resolution over parities. Itsykson and Sokolov allow resolution on opposite right-hand sides of the same linear form. In the normalized two-clause example, write the parents $A\lor(z+b=1)$ and $C\lor(z+d=1)$ with addition mod 2. The second semantically implies $C\lor(z+b=0)\lor(b+d=0)$. Resolving the latter with the first produces $A\lor C\lor(b+d=0)$. This gives a proof-system interpretation of the pair rule. The assertion that a collection of such clauses describes *all* projected assignments requires the separate two-witness argument. The paper studies refutations, including lower bounds with tree-like restrictions. This experiment measures the size of an exact projected function even when the input is satisfiable. [Itsykson and Sokolov, article definitions and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apal.2019.102722) · [Project's finite-set definitions, inspected checkout](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/00768105115b64a29ae775b0488cdfed951810d7/lean/PEqualNP/Defs/ProofComplexityLin.lean)

## Prior work on auxiliary variables

Andraschko, Danner and Kreuzer call the same affine-clause syntax XOR-OR-AND normal form (XNF). Definition 3.4 and Remark 3.5 require a bijection on solutions under projection in their encoding transformations. Propositions 3.13 and 3.15 show how to encode a product of linear forms with a fresh variable and two width-two XNF clauses, and how to iterate product substitution. Danner and Kreuzer subsequently characterize equivalence of two XNF clauses by linear algebra in Proposition 2.12 and discuss reduced representatives. These papers give direct precedents for the gate encoding and testing single-clause equivalence. A question left by those statements is the minimum clause count when at most one existential helper is available for the entire inner product. [Andraschko, Danner and Kreuzer, 2024, §3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11786-024-00594-x) · [Danner and Kreuzer, 2026, §2](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.20298)

Emdin, Kulikov, Mihajlin and Slezkin study the number of clauses in a pointwise existential encoding with a budget of guess bits. They bound the number and width of *ordinary* clauses for parity of input bits. In XNF the same parity predicate has one clause. The model closest to the present helper extension at the circuit level is OR of $2^s$ restricted affine-CNF copies obtained by fixing $s$ helper bits. Riazanov, Sofronova and Sokolov discuss the problem of an inner-product lower bound for a general OR of ANDs of ORs of parities in §1. A single guessed bit gives only two cofactors and imposes the additional requirement that they come from one formula. Their lower bounds concern a specified subclass. [Emdin et al., MFCS 2022](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2022.47) · [Riazanov et al., ITCS 2026, §§1, 3.3](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.112)

I searched for “existential CNF of parities”, “nondeterministic DNF of parities”, “CNF of parities auxiliary variables inner product”, limited nondeterminism in CNF encodings and XNF encodings of products. I found the exact zero-helper bound and the preceding adjacent results. I did not locate the tight one-helper inner-product clause tradeoff stated in this project. Its proof here is a derivation from the zero-helper bound, exact one-variable elimination and splitting the inner product. The search does not establish publication priority.

For the block-local theorem, each helper word fixes a Cartesian product of accepted sets on the visible blocks. This is a fixed-partition rectangle cover. Bova et al. define rectangular covers and derive covers from decomposable circuits. Mengel and Wallon use two-party rectangle covers to study width of ordinary CNF encodings with auxiliary variables. In this experiment, every Cartesian product in the cover can contain only one even block-parity pattern. Counting patterns gives the minimum helper budget. The additional claims at that budget use the affine-clause restriction within each visible block. The graph-flat argument gives the exact sum of local clause counts, and the equality conditions constrain the witness-to-pattern map to be affine at the clause optimum. Arbitrary relabellings are possible if only the number of helpers is minimized. A search on block-local encodings, rectangle covers, auxiliary variables and affine labels located the rectangle technique and the inner-product flat bounds, but did not identify the combined exact clause count or witness-label characterization in these papers. [Bova et al., IJCAI 2016, §2 and Theorem 6](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2016/147) · [Mengel and Wallon, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.05290) · [Cohen and Shinkar, §6 and Appendix A](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/099/download/)

The certificate bound by degree of a helper-slice indicator uses another established method. In Proposition 2, Ellis gives the Aharoni–Holzman argument that the indicators of an irredundant family of coordinate subcubes are linearly independent, by evaluating at one private point per member. He bounds their number by the dimension of the space of multilinear polynomials of the relevant degree. For the present application the slice may be an arbitrary affine flat. Over the binary field, multiplying its independent affine equality indicators still gives a polynomial of degree at most its codimension. This permits the same dimension argument and bounds the number of source clauses that can witness failure of extension. [Ellis, *Irredundant Families of Subcubes*, Proposition 2 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.2960)

## Scope of the cited lower bounds

- Bryant §2, Eq. (4) computes singleton existential quantification by OR of the two cofactors. The reduced OBDD is canonical for a fixed variable order. §5 explains memoization, identical-node sharing and the quantification algorithm. The size of an OBDD can grow exponentially. Darwiche and Marquis define singleton and arbitrary-set forgetting separately in Definition 5.7. Proposition 5.1 and Table 7 show that OBDD and fixed-order OBDD admit polynomial-time singleton forgetting but fail the corresponding arbitrary-set property. Their appendix gives a succinctness argument. These statements illustrate why a bound in the current representation size does not give a bound in the original SAT input length. [Bryant, §§2, 4–5](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bryant/pubdir/hmc-bdd18.pdf) · [Darwiche and Marquis, pp. 241–243, 258](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/project/jair/pub/volume17/darwiche02a.pdf)
- Itsykson and Ovcharov §2.3 specify a single current OBDD and a set of unread input clauses. A step conjoins one unread clause, projects a variable absent from unread clauses, or reorders the OBDD. Theorem 21, for the binary pigeonhole CNF with $h=2^\ell$ holes and $h+1$ pigeons, forces an intermediate OBDD of size at least $2^{\Omega(h^\varepsilon/\log h)}$, with $\varepsilon \approx 0.227092$ defined there. Their first family also gives a lower bound for a read-once nondeterministic branching-program model. Our representation stores a conjunction of independent clause descriptions and computes affine intersections and canonical row spaces. Applying their OBDD theorem to this algorithm would require a size-preserving translation and simulation of those steps. The inner-product flat-cover bound instead applies directly to this representation. [Itsykson and Ovcharov, SAT 2024, §2.3 and Theorem 21](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol305-sat2024/LIPIcs.SAT.2024.19/LIPIcs.SAT.2024.19.pdf)
- Haken's publisher abstract states a superpolynomial shortest ordinary-resolution proof bound for Cook–Reckhow pigeonhole tautologies. In refutation form the input is their negation. A general affine atom already exceeds the ordinary resolution line syntax. Haken's theorem gives no automatic bound on proofs using parity equations or algorithms using arbitrary projected representations. The publisher abstract was available for this pass, and we use only its stated scope. [Haken, Theoretical Computer Science 39 (1985), 297–308](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(85)90144-6)

The finite-set project semantics uses `LinEq V = Finset V × Bool`, with a parity evaluated on the indicated support, and `LinClauseSat` holds when some member equation holds. The separate search namespace uses lists of equations over `Fin n` and a derivation whose newest line comes first. Semantic weakening in both concerns implication between individual lines. For the flat representation, single-line implication reduces to reversed containment of forbidden flats by Gaussian elimination. General entailment by an entire mixed conjunction already includes unsatisfiability as the special case of an empty conclusion. The literature pass provides no polynomial-time algorithm for such global tests.
