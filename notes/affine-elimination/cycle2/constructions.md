# Two witnesses under cross-block affine dependencies

Write $v_i=(x_i,y_i)\in V_i=\mathbb F_2^{2b_i}$ and $q_i=x_i\cdot y_i$. An encoding of even inner product accepts a visible tuple $v$ exactly when some $h$-bit word $w$ avoids every listed forbidden affine flat. Assign each listed flat a home block. This note permits a flat to depend on variables at other blocks and on every helper. The two theorems use the assigned homes in different ways.

## A bound from the rank of foreign rows

For each block $i$, let $W_i$ be the span of the restrictions to $V_i$ of the linear coefficient rows defining **all** flats assigned to other blocks. Empty flats can be discarded first. This space is independent of the choice of equations for each nonempty flat, because any such list of defining rows spans its normal space. Write $c_i=\dim W_i$ and $K_i=\{u:\ell(u)=0\ \forall\ell\in W_i\}$. Define $2r_i$ as the rank on $K_i$ of the alternating polar form $B_i(u,v)=q_i(u+v)+q_i(u)+q_i(v)$. Let $I=\{i:K_i\ne0\}$.

**Theorem 1.** For every assignment of homes in every such encoding, $|I|\le h+1$. If $h\ge1$ and $|I|=h+1$, at least $2^{r_i}$ input clauses have home $i$ for each $i\in I$. In particular $m\ge\sum_{i\in I}2^{r_i}$. Also $r_i\ge b_i-c_i$.

For two helpers and three active blocks of total size $k$, this gives $m\ge3\cdot 2^{(k-\sum_i c_i)/3}$. On three balanced blocks with total foreign rank $o(k)$ all three are active for sufficiently large $k$. Thus an improvement in the constant $1/3$ of the exponent requires foreign rank of order $k$ in every such homing. This assertion allows any number of helper-rank-two flats. Four blocks each with a nonzero private direction already make an encoding with two helpers impossible for that homing.

**Proof.** On a product of cosets $\prod_i(a_i+K_i)$ all contributions to foreign constraints are constant. Every home group's clauses at a fixed $w$ now examine only their home block. The accepted set for this word on the slice is a Cartesian product. For each $i\in I$, choose nonzero $u\in K_i$. There exists $a_i$ with $q_i(u)+B_i(a_i,u)=1$ because $B_i$ is nondegenerate on $V_i$. Thus $q_i(a_i)$ and $q_i(a_i+u)$ are different. At each inactive block use one point. Every allowed parity vector on the active blocks now occurs, their sum being fixed by the inactive entries.

Every nonempty rectangle is monochromatic in the full block-parity vector, since changing one coordinate within it cannot flip total parity. For $|I|\ge1$ there are $2^{|I|-1}$ target vectors, and each requires a distinct word. This proves the first bound, including the vacuous case $I=\varnothing$. At equality of the word budget all words occur, one per parity vector, and every rectangle is the entire product of its prescribed local level sets. Otherwise a missing point would need a second word for the same parity vector.

Fix $i\in I$. Since $|I|\ge2$, choose two words whose $i$th prescribed parity bits are 0 and 1. Parametrize their affine line by $t\in\mathbb F_2$, making the prescribed bit $t$. The group of home-$i$ clauses on this line and its chosen affine slice describes $q_i(v_i)=t$. This follows by projecting the full rectangles onto $V_i$; every other factor is nonempty at either word. Every restricted forbidden flat is affine or empty.

In general let $K$ have dimension $d$, and let the polar rank of a quadratic on an affine translate of $K$ be $2r$. The graph $t=1+q(v)$ has $2^d$ points. A flat in this graph projects injectively to a flat in the $v$-space. On that projection $q$ agrees with an affine bit, since the inverse of the one-to-one affine projection is affine. Second differences show that the polar form vanishes on the projection's direction. Quotienting by its radical gives a symplectic vector space of dimension $2r$, in which a totally isotropic subspace has dimension at most $r$ by $U\subseteq U^\perp$. Hence the projected dimension is at most $d-r$, and each flat covers at most $2^{d-r}$ graph points. At least $2^r$ are needed. Finally $\dim K_i=2b_i-c_i$ and the radical of $B_i|_{K_i}$ embeds in $K_i^{\perp_{B_i}}$, which has dimension $c_i$. Therefore $2r_i\ge 2b_i-2c_i$. Summing and applying arithmetic–geometric mean gives the three-block claim.

The graph bound itself is exact. Symplectic pair elimination on the quotient produces an isotropic subspace of dimension $r$. Lift it and adjoin the radical to obtain an isotropic subspace of dimension $d-r$. On each of its $2^r$ cosets, all second differences vanish, and the graph of the quadratic over the coset is an affine flat.

The quantities above are directly checkable from a supplied assignment of homes. Reduce each flat's rows, restrict to each foreign block, compute the nullspace $K_i$ by Gaussian elimination, then take the rank of the pairing matrix on its basis. Choose an anchor by testing $q_i(u)$ and, if necessary, setting the coordinate paired with a nonzero coordinate of $u$. In dense encoding, put $S=2+n+m+L$ with $n$ total variables and $L$ raw rows. Each block uses at most $L$ checked independent input rows, and all matrices have at most $n$ columns. Gaussian elimination uses $O((L+1)(n+1)^3)$ bit operations by row scans. The matrix of pairings uses at most $n^2$ dot products of length $n$. Allowing another polynomial factor for loop indexing, sorting pivot lists and repeated input scans gives $O(S^7)$ bit operations and $O(S^4)$ working bits for all blocks. The theorem does not require optimization over homes.

**Dense example.** Start with the three-block parity-equality encoding on blocks of equal size $b$. In every clause simultaneously replace $x_1$ by $x_1+\mathbf1(\mathbf1\cdot x_2)$ and $y_2$ by $y_2+\mathbf1(\mathbf1\cdot y_1)$. The two new cross terms in the total inner product cancel. This invertible shear gives a valid encoding in which clauses may read every coordinate of a foreign operand vector. The foreign ranks are $(1,1,0)$ and half-polar ranks $(b-1,b-1,b)$. It uses $3\cdot2^b$ clauses, while Theorem 1 proves at least $2\cdot2^b$ for this rank profile.

## Exact optimum with a shared affine bit of the middle block

There are now three nonempty product blocks, two helpers, and a fixed nonconstant affine bit $s=\sigma(v_2)$ of the middle block. The middle home can read $v_2,w$. Each endpoint $i=1,3$ can read $v_i,s,w$. Its forbidden flats are arbitrary affine systems in those quantities. They may have any helper rank. Suppose $b_2\ge2$. Denote by $m_i$ the number of clauses assigned to block $i$.

**Theorem 2.** In this model the minimum total number of clauses is exactly $\sum_{i=1}^3 2^{b_i}$. More precisely, each encoding induces two bijections $p(s,\cdot)$ from witness words to even block-parity triples, one for each $s$. For **any** prescribed pair of such bijections write $p_i(s,w)$ for the label at block $i$, and let $\delta_i=1$ if the linear parts of $p_i(0,\cdot)$ and $p_i(1,\cdot)$ differ, zero otherwise. The exact minimum with these labels is
$$
2^{b_1+\delta_1}+2^{b_2}+2^{b_3+\delta_3}.
$$
Every summand is individually necessary for its home and simultaneously achievable. In particular the option of switching a witness labeling using dense affine information from another block can increase the cost of a prescribed labeling.

**Why the labels are forced.** For fixed $s$ all four even parity triples occur. Indeed a nonconstant affine hyperplane of $V_2$ has a restricted polar form of rank $2b_2-2>0$, since the symplectic orthogonal of its direction is a line contained in that direction. Therefore $q_2$ is nonconstant on each slice of $\sigma$. Each endpoint has both local levels available. The four helper rectangles on that slice are saturated. The rectangle proof from Theorem 1 gives a unique word per triple and the entire local level sets. Every permutation of the four elements of $\mathbb F_2^2$ is affine. To see this directly, the images of 0 and the two basis vectors are distinct; the differences from the first image are therefore linearly independent, determining an invertible affine map. The fourth image is forced. Identifying even parity triples with their first two coordinates shows that each label $p_i(s,\cdot)$ is a nonconstant affine function.

Write $\alpha(w)=p_i(0,w)$ and $\beta(w)=p_i(0,w)+p_i(1,w)$ for each home in turn. Its required bit equals $\alpha(w)+s\beta(w)$. Each endpoint's group must express precisely its local equality in $(v_i,s,w)$. Both switch values are attainable in physical inputs. The middle group must express precisely $q_2(v_2)=p_2(\sigma(v_2),w)$ because its other two factors are nonempty at each relevant $s,w$. The rectangle proof enforces these equalities even if an encoding was specified only by its existential projection.

**Endpoint counts.** If $\beta$ is constant, $z=\alpha(w)+s\beta$ is an independent affine coordinate. The local violating set is the graph $z=1+q_i$ times free coordinates. The graph bound above gives exactly $2^{b_i}$ flats, since fixing free coordinates gives a lower bound and extending optimally covering flats as cylinders gives the upper bound. If $\beta$ has nonzero linear part, its linear part and that of $\alpha$ are independent. Otherwise $\alpha+\beta=p_i(1,\cdot)$ would be constant. Relabel helpers by $z=\alpha(w)$ and $u=\beta(w)$. The violating graph is $z=1+q_i(v_i)+su$. It is the graph of inner product on $b_i+1$ pairs, giving exactly $2^{b_i+1}$ flats. One explicit optimum fixes the first operands of all pairs and imposes their resulting one affine equation on the output and second operands.

**Middle count.** Write $\sigma(v)=\kappa+B(a,v)$, possible because the middle polar form is nondegenerate. Translate the visible coordinate by $v'=v+a\beta(w)$, an invertible affine change of the combined visible-helper variables. Since $\beta^2=\beta$ as a Boolean function,
$$
 q(v')=q(v)+\beta(w) B(a,v)+q(a)\beta(w).
$$
The required equality thus becomes $q(v')=\alpha(w)+(\kappa+q(a))\beta(w)$. The right side is either $p_2(0,w)$ or $p_2(1,w)$ and is nonconstant affine. The same graph argument gives exactly $2^{b_2}$ flats. Substituting the coordinate change into the optimal cover preserves the allowed middle-home support. The three constructions together enforce exactly the prescribed witnesses. Choosing the same bijection at both values of $s$ attains the unconditional optimum.

**An irredundant family with many rank-two clauses.** Put $b_i=b\ge2$ and $\sigma=\bigoplus_j x_{2,j}$. Impose
$$
q_1=w_1+s w_2,\qquad q_2=w_2,\qquad q_3=w_1+(1+s)w_2 .
$$
The two endpoint linear parts change, and the prescribed-witness optimum is $5\cdot2^b$. The global witness is unique on even tuples, namely $w_2=q_2$, $w_1=q_1+s q_2$. Compile each endpoint by fixing its $x_i$ pattern and $w_2=t$, then forbidding one affine mismatch involving $w_1$. This produces $4\cdot2^b$ flats with helper rank two and $2^b$ middle flats of rank one. Its foreign ranks are $(0,1,0)$. As $k=3b$, this family is outside the earlier small-count condition $p=o(2^{k/4})$ on the number $p$ of rank-two inputs.

Every clause in this particular compilation is necessary even for the existential projection. For a selected endpoint clause, fix its prescribed $x_i$ and $w_2=t$, choose middle variables realizing $q_2=t$, and choose $w_1$ so the selected endpoint disagrees. Choose the opposite endpoint variables to realize that endpoint's target parity. Exactly the chosen clause fails and the visible tuple has odd total parity. For a selected middle clause, take its prescribed $x_2$, any $y_2$, and $w_2=1+q_2$. Choose $w_1$ freely and attain both required endpoint parities. Here again the selected clause is the sole failure on an odd tuple. Deleting it permits a spurious witness.

**Cost and checks.** For given block sizes, switch and pair of labelings, the compiler performs the changes above in bit rows. It enumerates each first-operand pattern, adding at most one extra bit at an endpoint. Each flat has at most $b_i+2$ rows of at most $2k+3$ bits, $k=\sum b_i$. The explicit masks, counters, affine substitutions, and output can be produced by row scans within $O((k+2)^4 m)$ bit operations and $O((k+2)^2 m)$ output bits, with $m$ the claimed clause count. Given an input encoding rather than prescribed labels, no algorithm to recover the labels or minimize arbitrary encodings is needed by the lower bound. The coefficient rank check and upper-bound compiler are implemented in the companion script.

From the research directory run the following command.

~~~sh
python3 cycle2/constructions.py
~~~

The script checks 72 distinct private subspaces (all subspaces in dimensions two and four) by brute-force quadratic-affineness and kernel enumeration. It verifies all visible points and all witness values for the dense shear at $b=1,2$. For the switch theorem it compiles all 576 pairs of bijections at block sizes $(1,2,1)$ and checks each joint visible/helper assignment, 589,824 in total. For each of the 36 pairs of nonconstant affine labels at an endpoint and at the middle, an independent enumeration of all flats in the local space verifies the matching volume lower bound. A separate deletion test checks every clause of the size-20 switched example at $b=2$ for projection essentiality. The saved output gives measured counts.

Both obstructions concern a prescribed interaction pattern or foreign-rank profile. The unrestricted two-helper optimum between exponents $k/4$ and $k/3$ is still open in this investigation. The proofs use the graph-volume argument and rectangle saturation from the original research cycle. Priority for these cross-block formulations is unverified.

## Complete witness fibers in four equal blocks

For $k=4b$ partition the product pairs into four blocks of size $b\ge1$. In this section a clause can involve **all** blocks, with arbitrary affine coefficients and both helpers. A word is uniform on an even four-bit parity pattern if it satisfies the encoding for *every* visible input having that pattern.

**Theorem 3.** If one helper word is uniform on two distinct even patterns, then $m\ge2^{2b-1}-1$. Consequently, below that clause count, for every partition into four equal blocks at least four of the eight even patterns have no uniform helper word. If one requires a uniform word for each even pattern, the optimum number of affine clauses is $\Theta(2^{2b})$. Witness sets may overlap and some accepted tuples may have additional input-dependent words. The same rate applies to the subclass in which the accepted predicate for each word depends only on the four block parities.

**Proof.** Fix the word $w$ and distinct even patterns $p,p'$. Their difference support $D$ has positive even size. Choose one distinguished position $j\in D$ and exchange $p,p'$ if necessary so $p_j=0$. The two groups $\{j\}$ and $D\setminus\{j\}$ are both odd in size. Make an affine substitution into the visible bits, using two independent pairs of operand vectors of lengths $b$ and $b-1$. Write $A,B$ for their inner-product bits. At the singleton position, use the first operand-vector pair as given, so the local parity is $A$. At every other position $i\in D$, copy the second operand-vector pair and pad both operands by one constant bit $p_i$. This gives local parity $B+p_i$. At inactive positions realize the fixed parity $p_i$ with constant vectors. Every physical input coordinate in the substitution is constant or a copy of one independent input coordinate.

When $A=B=0$ the pattern is $p$, and when $A=B=1$ it is $p'$. Both satisfy all clauses at the fixed $w$. When exactly one bit changes, an odd-sized group toggles, so the overall parity is odd. Soundness of the original existential encoding forbids *every* word there. Therefore the restricted conjunction represents exactly $A+B=0$, even inner product on $2b-1$ pairs. This also holds at $b=1$ when the second bit is identically zero. Each original affine clause remains one affine clause or a constant. The exact minimum of $2^{2b-1}-1$ for a flat cover of odd inner product now gives the desired lower bound. [Cohen and Shinkar, Theorem 1.5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/099/download/)

With four words and eight even patterns, if over four patterns have uniform witnesses, one word is uniform on two patterns. In particular this holds when every pattern has a uniform witness. Conversely encode $q_1=w_1,\ q_2=w_2,\ q_3+q_4=w_1+w_2$. Compile each equality by fixing the left operand vector in its region and forbidding the mismatch with the resulting affine row on right operands and helpers. This uses $2\cdot2^b+2^{2b}$ clauses and gives a uniform word per even pattern. Generating those dense rows takes $O((b+1)^4(2\cdot2^b+2^{2b}))$ bit operations by row scans. No enumeration over candidate witness colorings is necessary.

For $b=2$ the verifier checks the affine map on its zero and coordinate generators and independently on every input. It tests every unordered pair of even patterns, every subset of even patterns containing that pair, and every point on the section, 114,688 membership checks. The proof permits arbitrary behavior at other even patterns. In the shared-bit tests above, the program additionally checks all 30 nonconstant affine middle switches and all 36 middle label pairs at $b_2=2$, across 69,120 local assignments.
