# Two-helper bounds and related primary results

Let $q(x,y)=x\cdot y$ on $2k$ bits and $E=\{q=0\}$. Suppose a conjunction of $m$ affine clauses, with two additional bits, projects exactly onto $E$. Its four restrictions $C_w$ cover $E$, and each is contained in $E$. Pointwise expansion of the four-way failure condition into intersections of affine flats gives the direct bound $m^4\ge 2^k-1$. A balanced construction using three inner-product blocks has $O(2^{k/3})$ clauses. The following sources do not close the gap for unrestricted helper rank.

**A necessary dense one-sided approximator.** At least one cofactor accepts a fraction $\delta\ge(1+2^{-k})/8$ of all visibly assigned inputs, by counting the four accepted sets with multiplicity. The complement of this cofactor is a DNF of at most $m$ parity terms. It accepts *every* odd-inner-product point. On uniformly distributed inputs it agrees with the odd predicate with probability $\Pr[q=1]+\delta\ge5/8-3\cdot2^{-k}/8$. Thus an upper bound on the density of even points missed by a union of $m$ flats covering all odd points could improve the two-helper exponent. A bound for one arbitrary affine flat by itself cannot give this estimate, since overlaps in a union matter. Cohen and Shinkar prove the precise *zero-error* flat-cover bound and bounds for individual affine subspaces in §6 and Appendix A. [Cohen and Shinkar](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/099/download/)

Huang, Ivanov and Viola pose extraction by inner product on unions of polynomially many affine subspaces as Problem 7. Their Theorem 6 goes from a polynomial-size DNF of parities approximating inner product on a $1/2+1/\operatorname{polylog}n$ fraction of inputs to a polynomial-size constant-depth Boolean circuit with parity gates at its inputs for exact inner product (the class they call $\mathrm{AC}^0$-Xor). This is a conditional implication. The necessary approximator above is one-sided and uses the particular four cofactor structure. Their theorem gives no lower bound with exponent $k/3$ in this situation. Riazanov, Sofronova and Sokolov distinguish general OR–AND–OR–XOR circuits from the subclasses for which their 2026 paper proves size bounds. At most four disjuncts can arise by fixing the two helper bits. [Huang, Ivanov and Viola, §1.3 and Theorem 6](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/137/revision/1/download/) · [Riazanov, Sofronova and Sokolov, §1](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.112)

**An exact result for the opposite polarity when $k$ is even.** The even set has $2^{2k-1}+2^{k-1}$ points. A flat on which $q$ is constant has dimension at most $k$. Its direction is totally isotropic for the nondegenerate polarization of $q$. Thus at least $2^{k-1}+1$ flats cover the even set exactly when $k\ge1$. For even $k\ge2$ this is attained. The projective nonzero singular points of the hyperbolic form $q$ admit an orthogonal spread over $\mathbb F_2$ in this parity of dimension. The corresponding vector $k$-spaces cover the even set, meeting only at zero. Each contributes $2^k-1$ nonzero points, so their number is $(2^{2k-1}+2^{k-1}-1)/(2^k-1)=2^{k-1}+1$. Kantor gives the construction from a symplectic spread and a Desarguesian example. The existence of a projective spread requires even $k$. That restriction on a particular construction leaves the optimal cover by arbitrary affine flats for odd $k$ undecided here. This comparison concerns exact covers of one level set and gives no density bound for a union of flats that may also contain points of the opposite level. [Kantor, *Nondesarguesian planes, partial geometries, strongly regular graphs and codes arising from hyperbolic quadrics*, pp. 511–512](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/PlanesGraphsOrthSpreads.pdf)

The nearest limited-guess encoding lower bounds located for parity are for ordinary CNF. The extra flexibility of parity literals is material, since one such atom computes parity by itself. The exact-synthesis exercise here concerns finite values of $k$. It cannot determine the asymptotic two-helper exponent by extrapolation. [Emdin et al., *CNF Encodings of Parity*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2022.47)

## Every syndrome order has width at least $m-3$ for Hamiltonian-cycle encodings

**Theorem.** Let $m\ge4$. Give each directed edge $i\to j$, $i\ne j$, of the complete loopless digraph on $m$ vertices its own visible Boolean indicator $e_{ij}$. A finite affine-clause system on these and any number of Boolean helper variables is an exact encoding if projecting out all helpers gives exactly the incidence vectors of directed Hamiltonian cycles. For every such system and every variable order, at least one layer of the suffix-row-basis procedure has rank $w\ge m-3$. In fact, if $r(e)$ is the rank of the new suffix basis just after eliminating the visible edge $e$, then
$$
\sum_{e}2^{r(e)}\ \ge\ (m-1)\big[(m-2)2^{m-3}+1\big].
$$
This lower bound needs neither a size bound on the Boolean encoding nor a bound on the number of extensions of a tour. The visible bits must represent the individual labelled edges as above. It concerns the explicit syndrome recurrence and all of its orders.

**Conversion to a straight-line min-plus computation.** Fix the encoding and the order before assigning any edge costs. Assign arbitrary nonnegative costs $c_e$ to the directed edges, and use the semiring $(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\cup\{\infty\},\min,+,\infty,0)$. For a suffix syndrome $s$, fix any suffix assignment yielding $s$. The layer entry is the minimum cost of selected edges in the eliminated prefix over prefix assignments that satisfy every touched factor together with that fixed suffix. Touched factors see the suffix only through the recorded forms, so this value is independent of the representative. An empty set of feasible prefixes gives the entry $\infty$. Untouched factors are outside this entry until their introduction. The basis-coordinate property used by the Boolean DP ensures that for a trial pivot bit $b$ all newly touched clauses and the predecessor syndrome are determined by $(s,b)$. Thus infeasible choices and predecessor indices can be identified while constructing the circuit, independently of $c$. For each feasible choice use the predecessor value. Add $c_e$ only when the pivot is the visible bit $e$ and $b=1$. Take the minimum over the two choices. The starting entry is $0$ subject to variable-free constraints. This constructs a straight-line circuit with arbitrary wire sharing. The single last entry is the cost of a minimum directed Hamiltonian cycle for every assignment of costs, by induction on the eliminated prefix. Repeated witnesses have the same tour cost, so taking their minimum preserves the value.

At a helper step both branches have weight zero. Wire these directly, with a binary minimum if needed. At a visible edge step the $b=0$ branch also needs no addition. Only the $b=1$ branch can require an ordinary addition of two costs, at most one per new syndrome. Hence the number of tropical *multiplications*, i.e. ordinary additions, is at most $\sum_e2^{r(e)}$. Minima are gates of the other semiring operation. All row reductions, table indices and Boolean feasibility tests are fixed when constructing the straight-line circuit. The comparison theorem below bounds additions alone, regardless of the number of minima or the cost of finding the supplied encoding. Constants $0$ and $\infty$ are allowed. Self-loops, if present as extra coordinates of a matrix encoding, have zero weight and are never selected by a tour for $m\ge2$.

**Source and calculation.** Jerrum and Snir define the nonnegative min-plus semiring $M^+$ in §2, item (iv). In §4.4 they use independent directed edge costs and the polynomial indexed by full-cycle permutations. Its min-plus evaluation is the travelling salesman cost on the complete digraph. They prove that the number of semiring multiplications required by any straight-line computation is at least $(m-1)[(m-2)2^{m-3}+1]$, and exhibit a matching computation. Their conclusion explicitly includes $M^+$. They bound the function on *all costs*, which is exactly what the circuit above computes. There are $m(m-1)$ possible weighted steps. Therefore
$$
2^w\ \ge\ \frac{m-2}{m}2^{m-3}+\frac{1}{m}\ >\ 2^{m-4}\quad(m\ge4).
$$
Since $w$ is integral, $w\ge m-3$. This derivation uses a classical monotone lower bound; the paper supplies the straight-line lower bound and the preceding reduction supplies its rank interpretation. [Jerrum and Snir, *Some Exact Complexity Results for Straight-Line Computations over Semirings*, §2 and §4.4, pp. 890–891](https://snir.cs.illinois.edu/listed/J7.pdf)

**Connection with the formal VP/VNP artifact.** At the inspected commit, Lean's `hc` uses $\prod_i X_{i,\sigma(i)}$ for a permutation with one cycle on all vertices. `Computes` counts inputs, constants, additions and multiplications in a straight-line arithmetic computation. The paper's stated lower bound for this polynomial is over arbitrary fields. It uses the usual field addition for a sum over Boolean witnesses in `VNP`. If the encoding has one helper extension for each tour, replace $\min$ by field addition in the recurrence, tropical multiplication by polynomial multiplication, and each $c_e$ by $X_e$. Induction in the formal polynomial ring gives exactly `hc`, with one distinct squarefree monomial per tour. For a run on $t$ Boolean variables the circuit needs at most $2+m(m-1)+2t2^w$ gates including all edge inputs and the constants $0,1$. With multiple helper extensions, the coefficient of each monomial is its witness count in the field. For example, adjoining one free helper doubles every coefficient and gives zero in characteristic 2. The tropical argument remains valid even in that example. Combining the field transfer for unique extensions and polynomial $t$ with the artifact's stated lower bound would yield $w=\Omega((\log m)^{4/3})$ for all sufficiently large $m$ in fixed characteristic. The $m-3$ conclusion follows independently from the classical theorem above. This comparison was made by inspecting the artifact's statement and is not an independent verification of its Lean proof. This does not transfer a Hamiltonian *optimization* lower bound to plain SAT decision algorithms. The transfer works here because these specific state transitions can be weighted without consulting the edge costs for control flow. An algorithm that keeps one witness after satisfiability-preserving simplifications, or chooses branches by inspecting weights, need not admit this straight-line interpretation.

A related width lower bound is Mengel and Wallon's §6 argument for the Boolean permutation-matrix predicate, especially Lemma 27 and the discussion of clausal encodings following it. They obtain a linear obstruction via communication complexity. Their setting uses graph widths of ordinary CNF encodings and accepts arbitrary permutation matrices, including cycle covers with several components. The parameter here is the suffix rank of affine rows in a specified elimination procedure and the relation asks for a single cycle. The derivation above does not assert priority for width lower bounds for permutation-type relations. [Mengel and Wallon, pp. 22–23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.05290)
