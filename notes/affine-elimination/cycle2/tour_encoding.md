# The optimal syndrome rank for directed tour encodings

Let $m$ be the number of vertices and give each of the $m(m-1)$ labelled directed edges its own visible Boolean bit. Any number of existential helper bits is allowed. The true visible assignments must be exactly the incidence vectors of directed Hamiltonian cycles. Use the suffix-row rank of the elimination procedure: for a prefix of the supplied order, take all original affine-clause factors touched by the prefix, restrict their coefficient rows to the suffix, and take their binary rank. Write $w$ for the largest such rank.

**The minimum possible $w$, over both encodings and orders, is $\Theta(m\log m)$.** The upper bound uses a polynomial-size ordinary CNF with a unique extension for every tour. The lower bound permits any finite number of affine clauses and any witness multiplicities. Its external input is Zhang's October 2026 preprint bound on the largest 1-rectangle of bipartite matching connectivity. We use this result as a cited theorem; the experiments check the reduction, not the communication lower bound itself. A separate argument in [sources.md](sources.md) gives $w\ge m-3$ directly from Jerrum and Snir's 1982 min-plus circuit bound.

## Upper bound

Put $L=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ and assign each vertex $i$ a helper word $p_i$ of $L$ bits. Forbid binary codes at least $m$ and pin $p_0=0$. Require exactly one outgoing edge in every row of the adjacency matrix, using one at-least-one clause and pairwise at-most-one clauses. For each $e_{ij}$, each possible $a<m$, and each bit position, post the ordinary clause saying that $e_{ij}=1$ and $p_i=a$ imply that bit of $p_j$ equals the corresponding bit of $(a+1)\bmod m$.

Each assignment to the selected edges now determines a function from the vertices to themselves. A finite functional digraph contains a directed cycle. The label increments by one modulo $m$ on that cycle, so its length is a positive multiple of $m$. It therefore contains all $m$ vertices, giving a tour. A tour has exactly one extension, with labels its forward distances from vertex zero. Order the edge variables one whole source row at a time, followed by the helper words. Within any row the touched suffix coordinates are limited to the $mL$ labels and at most $m-2$ remaining edges in that row. Once all edges have been processed there are only labels. Thus $w\le mL+m-2$. There are $O(m^3L)$ clauses of width at most $\max(m-1,L+2)$ and $O(m^3L^2)$ literal occurrences. With binary identifiers the input uses $O(m^3L^3)$ bits and can be emitted within that many bit operations, using fixed-length words and elementary scans.

## A boundary of rank r gives at most 2^r rectangles

At any cut partition the visible edge bits by prefix and suffix. Some helpers may be on each side. Choose a basis $B$ for the suffix coefficient rows of all touched factors, and let $r=\operatorname{rank}(B)$. For every $s\in\mathbb F_2^r$ make one rectangle. The prefix side consists of visible prefix assignments that admit choices of prefix helpers satisfying all touched factors when the suffix forms have values $s$. Every touched row can be evaluated from this data. The suffix side consists of visible suffix assignments admitting suffix helpers that satisfy all untouched factors and give basis value $s$. Any witnesses for a matching pair can be concatenated. Conversely any full solution determines one of these rectangles. This gives a cover of the 1-inputs by at most $2^r$ products. Restrictions of the left and right visible assignments to smaller private domains preserve this bound. This argument also applies when one predicate on an individual side is difficult to decide.

## A matching-connectivity slice at the middle of any visible edge order

Color the first half of the directed edges red and the second half blue. Take the variable-order cut immediately after the last red edge. Ignore helper positions for this coloring. We need large private sets of red and blue matchings, even for a coloring whose monochromatic complete bipartite subgraphs are small. All logarithms in the count are binary. Let $k=\lfloor m/100\rfloor$ and take $m$ sufficiently large.

1. Greedily pick $k$ red directed edges, then $k$ blue directed edges, making *all* $4k$ endpoints distinct. Each color initially has $m(m-1)/2$ arcs. Each used vertex blocks at most $2(m-1)$ arcs of either color. Even after $4k$ used vertices, at least $m^2/4$ arcs of the requested color remain for all sufficiently large $m$. Dividing by the two internal ordering counts, there are at least $(m^2/4)^{2k}/(k!)^2$ pairs of matchings.
2. Give every vertex one of five roles: red tail, red head, blue tail, blue head, or unused. Some four fixed pairwise disjoint sets $A,B,C,D$ of size $k$ account for at least a $5^{-m}$ fraction of the pairs. Let $\mathcal A$ be all red bijective matchings $A\to B$ and $\mathcal B$ all blue bijective matchings $C\to D$. Their choices are independent. The bound $k!\le k^k$ gives
$$
\log\big(|\mathcal A|\,|\mathcal B|\big)\ge 2k\log k-O(m).
$$
3. Fix an arbitrary connector bijection $\lambda:B\to C$. Average the second connector $\mu:D\to A$ uniformly over all bijections. For any $\alpha\in\mathcal A,\beta\in\mathcal B$, the permutation $\mu\beta\lambda\alpha$ of $A$ is uniform. Exactly a $1/k$ fraction of permutations consist of one cycle. Choose a fixed $\mu$ attaining at least the average number of successful pairs. Replace one fixed connector arc with a fixed directed path through all unused vertices. This changes no cycle counts. All connector/path bits are constants whichever side owns them. Fix every remaining arc to zero except the two matching families. On the restricted private domains the visible graph is a tour if and only if $\mu\beta\lambda\alpha$ is one cycle. At least $|\mathcal A||\mathcal B|/k$ input pairs are tours.
4. Relabel the four vertex sets by $[k]$. The map from the two chosen matchings to a pair of permutations $(a,b)$ with $b^{-1}a$ equal to the induced permutation is a product of independent injective maps on each party. Explicitly, identifying $A$ and $B$ by a fixed bijection $\iota:B\to A$, let $a=\iota\alpha$ and $b^{-1}=\mu\beta\lambda\iota^{-1}$. Every accepting rectangle on the slice therefore maps to a 1-rectangle for the unrestricted permutation problem on two $k$-sets. The number of input pairs is preserved by this relabelling.

Zhang's §4 proves that for some absolute $\eta>0$, every such 1-rectangle has at most $2^{(2-\eta)k\log k}$ pairs, for all large $k$. This is the maximum-area bound in the proof of Theorem 4.1. Hence even the restricted slice, which retains at least $2^{2k\log k-O(m)-\log k}$ accepting pairs, requires at least $2^{\eta k\log k-O(m)-\log k}$ rectangles. Since $k=\Theta(m)$, its logarithm is $\Omega(m\log m)$. The syndrome rectangle cover proves the claimed lower bound for the original arbitrary order. The proof chooses connector *cost-independent constants* only to restrict a Boolean relation. It does not assume a greedy procedure for finding an optimum encoding or order. [Zhang, *Settling Nondeterministic Communication Complexity of Graph Connectivity*, §4, Theorem 4.1 and its rectangle-size proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32346)

A peer-reviewed predecessor already yields an all-order $\Omega(m\log\log m)$ lower bound through the same slice. Raz and Spieker prove this nondeterministic communication lower bound for the full two-perfect-matchings problem. To pass from a cover-number bound $C_k$ to a maximum-area bound, observe that simultaneous relabelling of the two vertex sets is transitive on all successful matching pairs. The translates of a single yes rectangle of size $s$ therefore cover all $T=k!(k-1)!$ yes pairs using at most $\lceil(T/s)(\ln T+1)\rceil$ copies, by the union bound for independently random translates. Thus $s\le O(T\log T/C_k)$. Applied to the restricted slice, the $2^{O(m)}$ loss is absorbed by $C_k\ge2^{\Omega(k\log\log k)}$. This already rules out the candidate $O(m)$ attainable width and does not rely on the October 2026 preprint. [Raz and Spieker, *On the “log rank”-conjecture in communication complexity*, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01192528)

The quantified model is important. Each visible bit encodes one specified edge. A dynamic program that indexes tours by the set of visited cities and current endpoint can use a different edge variable at the same stage on different paths. The theorem concerns one global edge order and an affine suffix basis at each cut. The obstruction applies to this representation for every choice of order. Other methods for deciding SAT or finding tours may use different states or different orderings on different paths.

## Reproducible finite checks

Run `python3 cycle2/tour_encoding.py` from the research directory. For $m=2,3,4$ the raw CNFs had 7, 47 and 114 clauses. The observed maximum ranks in row order were 2, 7 and 10. Over all visible edge masks there were exactly 1, 2 and 6 tours with unique witnesses. The checker evaluates all label assignments on every functional mask and verifies on the edge-only clause subsystem that every skipped nonfunctional mask is impossible regardless of labels. For $k=2,3,4$ it also enumerates all permitted red matchings, all permitted blue matchings and all connector bijections on seeded arc subsets. It checks the cycle criterion and the $1/k$ average with zero and two extra path vertices. For example with $k=4$ and two extra vertices, 11 red and 18 blue choices yielded 1,188 successful tests across all 24 connectors. The best fixed connector realized 50 tours, exceeding the average of 49.5.
