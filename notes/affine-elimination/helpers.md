# How much can an existential helper compress a residual?

Let $Q_k(x,y)$ mean $\sum_{i=1}^k x_i y_i=0$ over $\mathbb F_2$. An encoding with $h$ helpers and $m$ clauses means a conjunction $C(x,y,w)$ of $m$ explicit affine clauses such that, **for every** visible input, $Q_k(x,y)\iff\exists w\in\mathbb F_2^h\ C(x,y,w)$. The minimum counts clauses over any choice of affine forms. It allows unbounded clause width, sharing of the affine forms and arbitrarily hard searches for a good encoding. A flat's *helper rank* is the rank of the helper columns in an independent system of equations defining its false set. Equivalent systems for that flat have the same rank. An inconsistent false set can be discarded.

**With one unrestricted existential helper the minimum clause count is $\Theta(2^{k/2})$.** More generally, fix any integer $h\ge0$. Among encodings in which each individual flat has helper rank at most one, the minimum is $\Theta_h(2^{k/(h+1)})$ as $k\to\infty$. The constants can depend on $h$. For one helper the rank hypothesis holds for every affine clause. With zero helpers the known exact minimum is $2^k-1$. These statements concern the description of a specified residual as a function of all visible inputs. They give an obstruction even when a fixed number of additional existential bits is allowed.

## Lower bound by eliminating a block

Take $m$ flats with visible coordinates $v$ and helper coordinates $w$. Carry flats independent of $w$ through projection. For each other rank-one flat, row reduction gives a domain $D_j(v)$ (a conjunction of affine equations), one nonzero vector $a_j\in\mathbb F_2^h$, and an affine bit $f_j(v)$. The flat forbids exactly $a_j\cdot w=f_j(v)$ on its domain. Indeed, every equation with nonzero helper coefficients has the same vector in the helper columns. XOR one such pivot into the others to cancel those columns.

At a fixed point outside all carried flats, admissibility means solving $a_j\cdot w=1+f_j(v)$ for all active $j$ with $v\in D_j$. If this system is inconsistent, choose a maximal linearly independent subfamily of its normals. Its equations are simultaneously soluble for every right-hand side because the map given by independent rows is onto. Some remaining equation must have an incompatible right-hand side with its expression in those basis rows. Its normal and those of that expression sum to zero. Their right-hand sides sum to one. This certificate uses at most $h+1$ rows. Conversely every such certificate gives an inconsistency. No consistency claim is needed for inactive equations.

Enumerate every subset $S$ of two to $h+1$ distinct non-cylinder flats whose normals sum to zero. Add the forbidden flat
$$
\bigcap_{j\in S}D_j(v)\ \cap\ \left\{\sum_{j\in S}(1+f_j(v))=1\right\}.
$$
This construction plus the cylinders gives the exact projected relation. With $c$ cylinders and $g=m-c$ other flats, it emits at most $c+\sum_{s=2}^{\min(g,h+1)}{g\choose s}$ flats, including any redundant ones. Thus $2^k-1\le (m+1)^{h+1}$ follows from the inner-product cover theorem. For $h=1$ the bound sharpens to $c+{g\choose2}\le\max(m,{m\choose2})$. For $k\ge3$ the latter yields $m\ge\lceil(1+\sqrt{1+8(2^k-1)})/2\rceil$. [Cohen and Shinkar, Theorem 1.5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/099/download/)

## Matching upper bound in the exponent

For $k\ge h+1$, split the $k$ coordinates into $h+1$ blocks whose sizes differ by at most one. Let $q_j$ be the inner-product parity in block $j$. Require $q_j=w_j$ for the first $h$ blocks, and $q_{h+1}=\sum_j w_j$ for the last block. These equalities admit exactly one helper assignment when the visible input has even parity, and none otherwise.

For a block of size $b$ required to have parity $L(w)$, enumerate its $2^b$ possible left vectors $s$. Forbid $x_{\rm block}=s$ together with $s\cdot y_{\rm block}+L(w)=1$. This is one affine flat per $s$, with helper rank at most one. Hence $m\le(h+1)2^{\lceil k/(h+1)\rceil}$. For $h=1$ the explicit count is $2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}+2^{\lceil k/2\rceil}$. The one-helper construction has a useful computational property. After projecting its helper, same-block pairs have disjoint domains. Cross-block pairs yield one flat for each full $x=s\ne0$, precisely the optimum unassisted cover. The explicit upper bounds count all listed flats, including possibly inconsistent ones at $h=0$.

## Exact optimum when clauses respect the visible blocks

There is a stronger result for the block construction that allows arbitrary helper rank. Fix $B\ge2$ disjoint nonempty blocks of sizes $b_1,\ldots,b_B$ product pairs. Suppose each individual forbidden flat is a cylinder over all visible blocks except at most one. There may also be helper-only clauses. Such an encoding requires $h\ge B-1$ helpers. **At $h=B-1$ the exact minimum is $\sum_i2^{b_i}$ clauses** for this locality restriction, for all positive block sizes.

Fix a helper word $w$. Its nonempty accepted set is a Cartesian product of local accepted sets. On such a product every individual block's parity must be constant. Otherwise changing just that block could change the total parity to odd. Each of the $2^{B-1}$ even block-parity vectors is attained by visible inputs, so it needs a distinct witness word. This proves the budget bound. At equality all words are used, exactly one for each even pattern. Its rectangle contains the entire product of the corresponding block level sets because every point of that product needs a witness. Taking coordinate projections, the local conjunction for each block at that word is exactly its prescribed parity level set.

For block $i$, choose the witness $w_0$ for the all-zero pattern and $w_1$ for the even pattern with $i$ and one other position toggled. Substitute $w=w_0+t(w_0+w_1)$ in the clauses belonging to block $i$. On $(x_i,y_i,t)$ their conjunction is precisely $q_i=t$. None of the original helper-only clauses rules out a word. Each substituted clause still forbids at most one flat. The violating graph $t=1+q_i$ contains $2^{2b_i}$ points. Projection to $(x_i,y_i)$ is one-to-one on this graph. On the projection of any flat inside it, $q_i$ agrees with an affine function. Its direction is isotropic for the nondegenerate polar form of inner product, since all second differences there vanish. Its dimension is at most $b_i$, so a flat covers at most $2^{b_i}$ graph points. Consequently at least $2^{b_i}$ clauses belong to block $i$. Sum over the disjoint groups of clauses. The upper construction achieves equality for **any** block sizes. This also proves why simply assigning more intricate nonlinear roles to the same helper bits, while keeping each clause inside one visible block, cannot improve the balanced construction at the saturated budget. An improvement there would need a clause coupling different visible blocks.

At simultaneous equality of both budgets, the map from witness words to block parities must be affine. The proof exploits equality in the graph volume bound and the unequal sizes of the two inner-product level sets. [Rigidity and prescribed quadratic witness labels](rigidity.md)

## Algorithms, bit costs and limits

The routine in `helper_blocks.py` constructs the domains and inspects the subsets above on the original flat input. It rejects higher rank. It never searches for an extension or a minimum cover. For a total of $n$ original coordinates, a consistent independent flat contains at most $n$ packed rows of $n+1$ bits. Let $S=2+n+m+\text{number of input rows}$, let $T=1+\sum_{s=2}^{\min(g,h+1)}{g\choose s}$, and use dense input. A subset has at most $h+1\le n+1$ members and at most $(n+1)^2$ domain rows. Scanning those rows and reducing their affine system is polynomial in $S$ per subset. For example, even scanning a length-$n$ pivot list with a comparison sort per input row, and charging up to $O(S^2)$ bit operations for each key search with length-$O(S)$ keys and collisions, gives $O(S^7)$ per subset or initialization. The working width of a row stays $n+1$ bits. A direct sequential implementation can scan the input to collect each subset, reduce by indexed pivot scans, and append the consistent flat to the output stream, within $O(TS^7)$ bit operations. Retaining output takes $O((T+m)S^2)$ bits. These conservative bounds include enumeration and output. No global minimization is required. For the balanced upper construction, emitting every flat uses $O((h+1)2^{\lceil k/(h+1)\rceil}(k+h+1)^3)$ bit operations by direct row scans.

When $h$ is fixed the factor $T$ is polynomial in the *current* clause count. That count can already be exponential in the original SAT instance. With growing $h$, the displayed constants or exponents do not give an unrestricted polynomial bound. To evaluate a representation with genuinely unresolved helpers at a fixed visible point one may enumerate $2^h$ assignments and check all rows, or for the rank-one class solve the active linear system directly in polynomial time. The latter works at a fixed visible point and does not identify which visible point to try for SAT.

For arbitrary helper rank there is an elementary bound on the number of projected flats. At a bad visible point each of the $2^h$ fixed helper assignments violates at least one of the $m$ input clauses. Choose one such clause for each assignment. Their sections on the visible coordinates have affine intersection, which is entirely bad. Enumerating the $m^{2^h}$ choices covers exactly the bad set for $m>0$ (the empty input is identically true). Thus every unrestricted encoding still needs at least $(2^k-1)^{1/2^h}$ clauses. It is unknown in this investigation whether two unrestricted helpers permit asymptotically fewer clauses than the rank-one construction. The lower exponent available without further hypotheses is $k/4$ and the upper exponent is $k/3$. A sharper analysis of the four helper assignments proves the $k/3$ exponent necessary whenever the number of rank-two clauses is $o(2^{k/4})$. [Certificate classification](rank_certificates.md)

## Checks and prior work

`python3 helper_blocks.py` tests the collective projection on 512 seeded random systems, including dependent rows, constant domains, no helpers, cylinders and inconsistent flats, against explicit extension enumeration. It checks every visible input and every possible helper assignment for balanced instances through $k=5$, $h\le3$. It also compares the resulting canonical cover against the explicit unassisted optimum. For $k=5$, one helper uses 12 clauses and projects to 31, while two rank-one helpers use 10 and project to 31. The sources and the search for bounded-helper predecessors are recorded in [literature.md](literature.md). The exponent result is a derivation using known zero-helper bounds. Publication priority has not been established.
