# Affine measurements in XOR–AND circuits for Hamiltonicity

Let $H_N$ be directed Hamiltonian-cycle existence on $N\ge 2$ labelled vertices, with $d=N(N-1)$ loopless arc bits. In a Boolean circuit with XOR, binary AND, constants and fan-out, the input parities that reach the AND operands span **all $d$ input directions**. A second result gives a positive arithmetic circuit from a cover of the accepting inputs by affine systems. Its cost depends on the number of systems and on the ranks seen when scanning each system's columns.

## The input rank of the AND operands

Number the AND gates topologically and temporarily name their outputs $y_1,\ldots,y_t$. XOR and constants make the two operands of gate $j$ affine forms in $x,y_1,\ldots,y_{j-1}$. Form the binary matrix $A$ from the input coefficients of these $2t$ forms, omitting constants and coefficients of the $y_i$. If the final affine form has input part $\ell(x)$, then
$$
f(x)=\ell(x)\mathbin\oplus g(Ax).
$$
Indeed, fixing $Ax$ fixes every AND output in succession. All XORs, including those representing NOT as XOR with one, may be shared. This representation of XOR–AND circuits is standard in the study of Boolean multiplicative complexity. [Boyar and Find](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2018.02.023) · [Schnorr](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-51083-4_47)

For a Boolean function define $L(f)=\{a:f(x+a)+f(x)\text{ is constant in }x\}$, with additions over $\mathbb F_2$. This is a linear subspace, since the constants for two translations add under composition. Every $a\in\ker A$ belongs to $L(f)$. In fact, the smallest possible rank over representations of the displayed form is $d-\dim L(f)$. The constant derivative defines a linear functional on $L(f)$. Extend it to a linear functional $\ell$ on all inputs. Then $f+\ell$ is constant on cosets of $L(f)$, so it factors through a quotient map with that kernel. An individual circuit can produce a higher rank. [Lai, *Additive and linear structures of cryptographic functions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-60590-8_6)

For a monotone function a period $a$ makes every coordinate in its support irrelevant. Fix all other bits. The assignments with all moved bits zero and all moved bits one have equal values by periodicity. Monotonicity puts the entire interval at that value. An anti-period, with constant derivative one, makes every coordinate *outside* its support irrelevant. Along any edge increasing an unmoved bit, both the original and translated assignments increase. Since their values are complementary, neither value can change. Thus a monotone function essential in all coordinates can have a nonzero constant derivative direction only if complementing every bit always complements its output.

Removing any arc from a graph consisting of one tour destroys the tour. Since each arc belongs to a tour, every arc is essential for $H_N$. For $N\ge3$, the graphs of a clockwise tour and its reverse are edge-disjoint, so the clockwise graph and its full complement both contain a tour. For $N=2$, the graph with just one arc and its complement both lack a tour. Hence $L(H_N)=\{0\}$ and $\operatorname{rank}A=d$. This yields $t\ge\lceil d/2\rceil$. For $N\ge19$ the known OR restriction already yields the stronger numerical AND count $d-2\lceil 3N/2\rceil-1$. The rank result determines which input measurements any nonlinear computation must collectively distinguish. [Earlier degree and gate-count bound](degree-literature.md)

For a general circuit of rank $r$ and size $S$, a compiler can compute its canonical multilinear polynomial over any field. Choose a basis for the rows of $A$ and the coefficient vector of $\ell$, of total size at most $r+1$. Scan the input bits, keeping the syndrome of the processed prefix. At bit $i$, the zero transition has weight $1-X_i$ and the one transition has weight $X_i$ and adds column $i$ to the syndrome. At the end sum the weights of the accepting syndromes. Their Boolean outputs can be precomputed on representatives by evaluating the original circuit. Each assignment contributes its product of distinct literal weights once, so this is multilinear and agrees on the cube. The arithmetic cost is $O(d2^{r+1})$, with polynomial linear algebra and at most $2^{r+1}$ evaluations of the Boolean circuit to identify the accepting states.

## Affine accepting transcripts and trellises

A finer construction can use a different system of parity equations on each branch. Process the AND gates of a given circuit in order, expressing their operands as affine input functions after previous branch choices. Branch on the value of the first operand. Its product becomes zero on the zero branch and equals the second operand on the one branch. At the end impose the affine equation that the output is one. Discard inconsistent systems. Every accepting input follows exactly one resulting transcript. The compiler below also applies to any supplied disjoint cover of the accepting inputs by affine cells. Its size bound counts the gates of the constructed arithmetic circuit. A branch search can spend additional time finding and rejecting inconsistent or unneeded branches.

For a consistent cell $Mx=b$ choose any column order. At a cut write $P=\operatorname{im}M_{\rm prefix}$ and $Q=\operatorname{im}M_{\rm suffix}$. The extendible prefix syndromes form $P\cap (b+Q)$, a nonempty affine translate of $P\cap Q$. Their number is therefore exactly $2^{\lambda_i}$, where
$$
\lambda_i=\operatorname{rank}M_{\rm prefix}+\operatorname{rank}M_{\rm suffix}-\operatorname{rank}M.
$$
Keep those states only. From a retained state try each bit and check extendibility by elimination against the remaining suffix columns. With $w=\max_i\lambda_i$, the weighted sum for the cell uses $O(d\,2^w)$ arithmetic gates. Independent equations fixing individual coordinates can have full rank and $w=0$, with exactly one extendible state at every cut. The state formula is the conventional linear-code trellis dimension, equivalently a represented matroid connectivity function. The column order can vary by cell. [Kashyap, *Matroid Pathwidth and Code Trellis Complexity*](https://doi.org/10.1137/070691152)

For cells $t=1,\ldots,L$ of widths $w_t$, the arithmetic cost is $O(d\sum_t 2^{w_t})$. The signed weights above give the canonical polynomial for a disjoint cover. Positive weights $(1,X_i)$ instead give $\sum_{x:f(x)=1}\prod_{i:x_i=1}X_i$. Hamiltonicity's minimum accepted weight is $N$, so its lowest homogeneous component is the formal tour polynomial, with coefficient one per directed tour. Positivity permits the lowest component to be retained gate by gate. Products multiply lowest components. Sums retain the components of smallest degree among the nonzero inputs.

An overlapping affine cover contained in the accepting inputs still yields exactly the same tour *support* in its lowest component, with each coefficient equal to the number of covering cells at that tour. Over the nonnegative integers those coefficients survive. The rectangle count for positive circuits on tour support hence gives
$$
d\sum_{t=1}^{L}2^{w_t}\ \ge\ 2^{\Omega(N)}.
$$
In particular $\log_2 L+\max_t w_t=\Omega(N)$. This cover bound holds for both contains-tour and exact-tour acceptance. Disjointness is required for the literal-weight sum to represent the Boolean indicator over every field. For example, the two cells $x_1=1$ and $x_2=1$ count the all-one assignment twice, and their sum vanishes there in characteristic two. [Tour-support rectangle proof](decision-treewidth.md) · [Jerrum and Snir, Section 4.4](https://snir.cs.illinois.edu/listed/J7.pdf)
