# The two-window inequality L-meta-1596

The frozen desk statement uses `PEqualNP.Defs`. [Statement](statement.txt) · [Desk](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-1596) Write V for the input indices, Y for the first-negative witness, k=|Y|, and s for the gate count. Let T denote the rows meeting Y and D the other rows. Set w_i=min(k,|S_i|) on T and w_i=|S_i| on D. For a gate g, L(g) is its accepted singleton set, and H(g) is its accepted set of extensions Y∪{p}, indexed by p outside Y. A refund gate has L(g)=S_i and H(g)=S_j for some j in D. Let R be their number. The question is whether Σ w_i≤s+m+R.

`WindowComparison.lean` proves in Lean that checked desk rows 270, 529, 669, 1594, and 1595 imply the equivalence of L997 and L1596. Its statements are taken verbatim from the desk catalog. Neither local file proves either open conjecture. The admissible circuit in `mixed_gate.py` refutes the proposed intermediate implication “a gate charged in both windows is a refund.”

## Partial bounds

Every row with at least two points has a genuine gate as output. Its accepted singleton set is exactly the row. These output gates are distinct by linearity. A disjoint nontrivial row's output is always a refund, so R≥H, where H denotes the number of disjoint rows of size at least two.

* If all touched quotas are at most two, charge each touched quota above one to its output gate. Use the supplied heavy charges for the disjoint rows. Those charge sets are pairwise disjoint by linearity. If a touched output occurs in a heavy charge, its heavy support, all V∖Y, must equal that heavy row. This output is refunded. This includes k≤2.
* If all disjoint rows have size at most three, take the supplied light charges for touched rows (k≥2). Their charge sets are pairwise disjoint. The output of any nontrivial disjoint row is outside them because its singleton support shares at most one member with each touched row. Its gate and its refund pay up to two units. For k≤1 the preceding case applies.
* For k≥2, it suffices that Σ_i (w_i−3)_+≤ k−1+H. The two disjoint families of circuit gates used for this bound are described below.

### The two bounded-fan-in arguments

The mixed-row extension of upstream L-meta-1378 allocates a distinct output to every row of size at least two, and a distinct private parent to every row of size at least three. Drop rows of size zero and one first. Outputs and private parents carry a role unique to a single row. Every role either (i) accepts at least two singletons and only accepts inputs meeting the row, (ii) accepts every input meeting the row and rejects its complement assignment, or (iii) equals the negation of the row OR. Distinctness follows from pairwise intersections of size at most one. A row output carries (ii). The parent argument for (i)–(iii) is that an OR of two children covering at least three accepted points has a child covering at least two, an AND has a child rejecting the complement assignment, and a NOT must negate the row OR. Therefore at least Σ_i (min(3,|S_i|)−1)_+ gates carry roles even with arbitrarily many NOT gates. The finite injection is in `WindowSmall.lean`, adapted from the checked upstream L-meta-1378 proof, keeping its private helpers.

Here is a separate collection of k−1 gates. The designated t must be a real NOT: otherwise the network is monotone, unable to accept zero but reject Y at that wire. The first predecessor a of that gate is a real pre-NOT wire, because the NOT accepts zero and rejects Y; the upstream evaluator returns false for any missing or premature predecessor. Its monotone function h accepts Y and no input of weight <k. Build a connected proof DAG for h(Y)=1. Stop at every wire accepting at least one singleton. At OR choose a predecessor accepting Y; at AND follow every predecessor. Nullary true ANDs can be terminals as well, although at k≥2 they already accept all singletons. An input wire accepting Y is a singleton-accepting terminal; the false sentinel cannot accept. Select one accepted singleton from each stopped wire. The union of those points makes the root true, because the stopped wires are monotone and the proof choices force the root. Hence there are at least k distinct stopped wires. Any connected rooted DAG with t terminal vertices and I nonterminal vertices of fan-in at most two has I≥t−1 by counting edges against vertices. Its internal gates are thus a separate family of at least k−1. By the stopping rule each has empty singleton support and, being pre-NOT monotone with nonempty input domain, rejects zero. Such a gate carries none of (i)–(iii). The conclusion follows by counting the two families and R≥H.

### All instances with n≤9

For k≤2 use the touched-quota bound. For n−k≤3 use the disjoint-row bound. The latter also settles any remaining case in which no disjoint row has size at least four. Otherwise:

* For k=3 one has n−k≤6. At most one disjoint row has size at least four, since any two such rows inside six points meet twice. Its contribution to Σ(w_i−3)_+ is at most three, while k−1+H≥3.
* For k=4 one has n−k≤5. At most three rows have size at least four, since four four-point sets of pairwise intersection at most one have union of size at least 16−6=10. Each such row contributes at most one, apart from a possible disjoint five-point row contributing one further unit. Such a row supplies H≥1, giving the required bound.
* The only remaining k is five, with n=9. There must be a disjoint four-point row B=V∖Y. Every touched row has at most one point outside Y. A touched row of size at least four therefore has at least three points inside this fixed five-point set. At most two such touched rows can exist, by the same union bound. A touched row of size at least five already has at least four points inside Y, excluding a second touched row of size at least four. Together touched rows contribute at most two, and the heavy four-point row contributes one. This is below k−1+H.

This establishes the small-size cases mathematically. The Lean files check the frozen-statement equivalence and mixed-row injection; the threshold DAG and n≤9 corollary above are written proofs.
