# A necessary condition on signed Boolean proofs of Hamiltonicity

Suppose a fan-in-two AND/OR/NOT Boolean circuit decides directed Hamiltonicity on $n\ge3$ vertices. There are $M=n(n-1)$ distinct arc bits. Push negations to the inputs by constructing each gate and its complement once, with De Morgan's laws. This gives an AND/OR DAG with signed input literals, at most twice the original logical gate count, and the same Boolean output. Fold constants and bypass copies. Write $S$ for the resulting size including its literals. The analysis allows arbitrary depth and sharing.

Unfold an output **parse** by selecting one child at each OR and both children at every AND. Different occurrences of a shared gate can make different choices. Let $P$ be its set of positive edge labels, $D$ its set of negative labels, and $R$ the set of positive labels appearing at least twice. A parse is satisfied by an assignment $T$ precisely when $P\subseteq T$ and $D\cap T=\varnothing$. In particular every satisfiable output parse has a positive support containing a tour: the assignment $P$ itself satisfies it. There are $(n-1)!$ minimal accepting assignments, one per directed tour. On any parse satisfied by such a tour $T$, we have $P=T$.

**Tradeoff.** Choose a set $B$ of $b$ arc labels. For every output parse with $|P|\le n$ and with $P$ containing no tour, require that $B$ contain at least one label in $P\cap D$. This is a hypothesis about the internal proof of acceptance. For $0\le r\le M-b$, let $f_r$ be the fraction of all directed tours $T$ for which some parse satisfied by $T$ has $|R\setminus B|\le r$. For a universal constant $C$,
$$
f_r\ \le\ \min\left(1,\
 \frac{C(S+M+1) 2^{b+r}\binom{M-b}{r}}{H_n}\right),\qquad
H_n=\min_{n/3\le j\le 2n/3}\frac{(n-1)!}{(j-1)!(n-j-1)!}=2^{\Omega(n)} .
$$

In particular, if a polynomial-size Boolean circuit has such a set with $b=o(n)$, all but an exponentially small fraction of tours require $\Omega(n/\log n)$ distinct positive labels to repeat in **every** satisfied parse. More precisely one can take a sufficiently small positive constant times $n/\log_2 n$ in the displayed inequality. Repetition counts refer to occurrences in the unfolded proof, even if a DAG wire stored its bit once. This is a necessary condition on how a small Boolean circuit could recognize the actual Hamiltonian tours. A general Boolean circuit is free to have many contradictory parses or heavy repetition.

## Why conditioning keeps the computation positive

For each of the $2^b$ assignments $z$ to $B$, replace each signed literal on $B$ by its Boolean value. Replace each positive literal outside $B$ by its variable, and each negative literal outside $B$ by the scalar one. Evaluate the original DAG over the nonnegative integers using arithmetic addition for OR and multiplication for AND. Multiply the output by the squarefree monomial on the labels of $B$ assigned one and add the outputs of all slices. The result has a monotone arithmetic circuit with $O(2^b(S+M+1))$ slots. Constructing it never requires enumerating parse trees. Its correctness statement assumes the guard set; it supplies no efficient algorithm to discover or certify a good one.

Consider a term supplied by a parse surviving the substitutions. Its support is the union of the original positive labels outside $B$ and the selected labels in $B$. This contains the original $P$. If the resulting term has degree at most $n$, then $|P|\le n$. If $P$ contained no tour, the guard hypothesis would have put both signs of one label in $B$, which would have killed this parse in every slice. Thus every term of degree at most $n$ has degree exactly $n$ and is a squarefree tour monomial. A surviving inconsistent parse whose positive support already contains a tour is harmless to this conclusion.

For a tour $T$ and a satisfied parse with $R\subseteq B$, choose $z=T\cap B$. All literals on $B$ are satisfied, and the positive literals outside $B$ are exactly $T\setminus B$ with multiplicity one. This contributes the monomial $X_T$. Thus the degree-$n$ support includes every tour with such a parse. Coefficients can exceed one; only their supports enter the counting argument. If $B$ hits every inconsistent output parse, the converse holds too: each surviving degree-$n$ term comes from a parse satisfied by that tour and with no positive repetition outside $B$.

## Counting how many tours the circuit can certify

If there are any degree-$n$ terms, keep the lowest homogeneous part of each nonzero gate. Positivity lets us discard all summands of larger minimum degree and multiply the lowest parts of products. The resulting circuit uses no additional slots, and its output support is a subset of the tours. In a homogeneous monotone circuit every output parse can mark a gate of degree $j\in[n/3,2n/3]$. Fixing the gate monomial, or fixing the complementary part of the parse, bounds the two sides of this product by $(n-j-1)!$ and $(j-1)!$, respectively. The count only uses that every cross product is a spanning tour, so it holds for an arbitrary subset of the tour family. Therefore a circuit with $s$ slots can cover at most the fraction $s/H_n$ of all tours. [Counting proof](decision-treewidth.md#why-positive-weights-strengthen-the-bound)

This proves the bound for $r=0$. For $r>0$, enlarge $B$ by each $r$-element set of additional labels in turn. Enlarging the set preserves the conflicting-literal hypothesis. Every tour counted by $f_r$ has a parse whose repeated labels are covered by one of the enlarged sets. The union bound over their degree-$n$ supports gives the displayed inequality. A sharper form selects a uniform additional set of $u$ labels, with $r\le u\le M-b$. Every such tour is then covered with probability at least $\binom ur/\binom{M-b}r$. In the bound above one may replace $2^r\binom{M-b}r$ by the minimum of $2^u\binom{M-b}r/\binom ur$ over this range.

## The point where this approach stops

The signed parse model includes ordinary unrestricted Boolean circuits after the size-linear negation transformation. Proving that every small Hamiltonicity circuit admits both a small guard set and sufficiently many low-repetition proofs would contradict the bound, and would indeed establish a Boolean circuit lower bound. Neither property follows from circuit size alone in this proof. Adding a dead disjunct $x\land\neg x$ introduces an inconsistent parse, and duplicating an AND operand can create repetitions without changing the Boolean function. A size argument would have to find a suitable equivalent circuit or control such parses in a more invariant way.

There is also a barrier that persists after changing to an equivalent circuit. The proof only needs the predicate to reject every tour-free input of at most $n$ arcs and to accept every individual tour. These conditions hold for the deterministic logspace exact-tour language. For that language *every* polynomial-size decision circuit faces the same disjunction between guards and repetition, even if it has been resynthesized. Thus a general simplification theorem for polynomial-size circuits and these minimal inputs cannot finish the Hamiltonicity lower bound. An argument specific to the full decision predicate would have to exploit what it accepts on larger graphs. [Easy comparison language](easy-dense-layers.md)

This argument concerns the positive expansion of a specified Boolean DAG. The earlier signed interpolation argument concerns monomials surviving cancellation in a formal arithmetic circuit. Here the allowance for guards and nondeterministic choice of an accepting parse per tour permits overlaps even in the portion of the circuit being used. Circuits that are already decomposable are a special case.
