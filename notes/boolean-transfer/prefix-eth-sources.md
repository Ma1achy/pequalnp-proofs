# Superlogarithmic Hamiltonicity prefixes under ETH

**Conditional theorem.** Let $k(m)$ be nonnegative integers with $k(m)/\log_2 m\to\infty$. Suppose a single deterministic polynomial-time language agrees with directed Hamiltonicity on all sufficiently large $m$ through every degree up to $m+k(m)$ in their canonical Boolean polynomials over $\mathbb F_2$. Then the 3-SAT exponential-time exponent $s_3$ is zero. Consequently the positive-exponent Exponential Time Hypothesis rules out this uniform comparison language. The function $k$ need neither be monotone nor be supplied as input.

The degree-prefix equivalence says exactly that the algorithm is correct on every $m$-vertex digraph having at most $m+k(m)$ arcs. We use full labelled adjacency matrices of $O(m^2)$ bits. This is a decision guarantee and uses no Hamiltonian-cycle counting oracle. [Prefix equivalence and forced-path padding](degree-prefix.md)

**Size of the SAT reduction.** For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, Impagliazzo, Paturi and Zane decompose an $n$-variable 3-CNF into at most $2^{\varepsilon n}$ disjuncts, each with at most $C_\varepsilon n$ clauses, in $2^{\varepsilon n}\operatorname{poly}(n)$ time. Reading an explicitly redundant input adds polynomial time in its bit length. They also explicitly identify the usual reduction from 3-SAT measured in clauses to Hamiltonian Circuit measured in edges as a strong many-one reduction, and state the sparse Hamiltonian Circuit consequence at the end of §3. Their definition of “strong” gives a linear bound on the target parameter. [IPZ, *Which Problems Have Strongly Exponential Complexity?*, definition p. 516, Corollary 1 p. 523 and concluding paragraph of §3 p. 526](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~russell/ipz.pdf)

For an explicit directed construction with both bounds, start with a disjunct having $r$ clauses. Delete tautologies and repeated literals, and handle empty clauses and empty formulas directly. Replace each literal occurrence with its own variable, preserving its sign. For an original variable with at least two copies, add a cyclic chain of implications from each copy to the next. This enforces equality and adds at most $3r$ clauses in total. Every new variable occurs in at most three clauses. To obtain exactly three distinct variables per clause, replace a two-literal clause $D$ by $D\lor z$ and $D\lor\neg z$ for a fresh $z$. Replace a unit clause analogously using all four sign patterns of two fresh variables. These are equisatisfiable replacements. The resulting variable and clause counts $n',r'$ are $O(r)$, and the maximum number of occurrences of any variable is at most $c=12$.

Goldreich’s directed construction gives a graph with $v=2+(3c+3)n'+r'$ vertices, at most three incoming and three outgoing arcs per vertex, a source $s$ without incoming arcs and a terminal $t$ without outgoing arcs. It has a Hamiltonian path exactly when the formula is satisfiable. Add $t\to s$. The arc is forced in every spanning cycle and closing or opening it preserves the decision answer. The new arc leaves both degree bounds intact, so the number of arcs $e$ satisfies $e\le3v$. Both $v$ and $e$ are $O(r)$ and the reduction is computable in polynomial time in the explicit formula length. In particular, each sparse disjunct gives $v,e\le B_\varepsilon n$ for a fixed $B_\varepsilon$. [Oded Goldreich, *On Testing Hamiltonicity in the Bounded Degree Graph Model*, rev. 2, §2, pp. 5–6, construction, degree bound and decision equivalence](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2020/109/revision/2/download/#page=5)

**Padding and the exponent.** Fix a rational $\eta>0$ and let $M=2^{\lceil\eta n\rceil}$. Eventually $M\ge v$, and $k(M)/\log_2 M\to\infty$ gives $k(M)\ge B_\varepsilon n$ for every sufficiently large $n$. Split one graph vertex into a forced directed path to bring the graph to exactly $M$ vertices. This preserves Hamiltonicity and adds exactly $M-v$ arcs. Its excess is still $e-v\le B_\varepsilon n\le k(M)$, so the assumed language decides each disjunct. Writing the padded matrix and invoking the fixed polynomial-time algorithm cost at most $\operatorname{poly}(n)M^D$ per disjunct for some fixed $D$. The total exponential rate is at most $\varepsilon+D\eta$, up to polynomial factors. For every requested $\delta>0$, choose fixed $\varepsilon,\eta>0$ with $\varepsilon+D\eta<\delta$. A finite size threshold may be hardwired and smaller inputs solved by brute force. This gives a correct deterministic algorithm with rate at most $\delta$ for each $\delta$, hence $s_3=0$ by its definition as an infimum. [Impagliazzo and Paturi, *On the Complexity of k-SAT*, §1, p. 368](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/ImpagliazzoPaturi_2001_jcss.pdf#page=2)

**Uniformity and inverse bounds.** The fixed-rate argument uses $k$ only to prove eventual correctness and works even without an algorithm for finding its growth threshold. If the test $k(M)\ge e-v$ can be performed in $M^{O(1)}$ time, there is also an explicit single padding algorithm: enumerate powers of two $M\ge\max(v,M_0)$ until the test succeeds, where $M_0$ is the fixed onset of prefix agreement. For graphs with $v,e=O(n)$ the first such $M$ satisfies $\log M=o(n)$, since for every constant $\eta>0$ the trial $2^{\lceil\eta n\rceil}$ eventually suffices. The search and padded call thus cost $2^{o(n)}$ on sparse inputs. For $k(M)=\lfloor(\log_2 M)^a\rfloor$ with fixed $a>1$, one can use $\log_2 M=O(n^{1/a}+\log n)$ directly. Polynomial-size *nonuniform* circuits would supply advice at the padded lengths. The deterministic ETH consequence above requires a uniform language. An unbounded limsup of $k(m)/\log_2m$ alone does not give the eventual padding guarantee used here.
