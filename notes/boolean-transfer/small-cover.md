# Leaving all arcs incident with a fixed vertex formally unrestricted

Let $n\ge3$, let $A$ be the arc coordinates where Boolean identities are allowed, and write $F$ for the other arcs. Use $Y$ for the variables in $A$ and $Z$ for those in $F$. The ideal $D$ is generated by the squarefree unions of pairs of distinct directed spanning tours, and $B_A$ by the polynomials $Y_e^2-Y_e$. Assume there is a size-$s$ Boolean circuit that rejects graphs with zero tours and accepts graphs with one tour. Its values on graphs with several tours are arbitrary. [Definitions and promise equivalence](unique-promise-ideal.md)

**Conditional construction.** If every arc in $F$ has an endpoint in a set $C$ of $h$ vertices, an ordinary arithmetic circuit over $\mathbb F_2$ of size
$$
 O\!\left((n-1)^{2h}(s+2h+1)\right)
$$
computes a polynomial $P$ satisfying $P-h_n\in D+B_A$. All $Z$ variables remain formal. The hypothesis is a Boolean circuit family, so the bound holds nonuniformly. A constant $h$ and polynomial $s$ give polynomial size.

For a tour $T$ write $S=T\cap F$. Its **pattern** $S$ is determined by the predecessor and successor of each vertex in $C$. Thus there are at most $(n-1)^{2h}$ distinct patterns, each of size at most $2h$. For a fixed pattern $S$ restrict the promise circuit to graphs built as follows. Keep the input bits for the arcs in $A$, allow exactly the arcs $S$ from $F$, and delete every other outgoing arc at the tail of each edge in $S$. A tour in the restricted graph must use every edge in $S$. For each Boolean setting $y$ of the guarded arcs, its tours are exactly the tours with pattern $S$ whose $A$-edges are present in $y$.

Let $G_S(Y)$ be the ordinary arithmetic translation of this restricted circuit, and form
$$
                       P(Y,Z)=\sum_S Z_S G_S(Y),\qquad Z_S=\prod_{e\in S} Z_e .
$$
The construction uses $O(s+|S|+1)$ slots per distinct pattern. To list candidates, enumerate the local predecessor and successor choices, retain the selected edges in $F$, and deduplicate the resulting sets. Candidates requiring two outgoing arcs at one vertex can be discarded. Any remaining unrealizable candidate gives a restriction with zero tours at every Boolean $y$ and contributes zero modulo $B_A$. This avoids needing a tour oracle to filter the list.

## Why the congruence holds with formal arc variables

Specializing $Y=y$ for every Boolean $y$ gives the ring isomorphism
$$
 \mathbb F_2[Y,Z]/B_A\ \simeq\ \prod_{y\in\{0,1\}^{A}}\mathbb F_2[Z].
$$
Interpolation by the indicator of each assignment gives the inverse. The image of $D$ in this quotient is the product of the specialized ideals $D_y$. Indeed, each fiber ideal is generated by the specialized pair-union generators, and its multiples can be placed in that fiber alone by multiplying by the assignment indicator.

Fix $y$, and let $k_S$ be the number of tours with pattern $S$ surviving there. The coefficient of $Z_S$ in $h_n(y,Z)$ is $k_S\bmod2$. The Boolean promise fixes $G_S(y)$ to this coefficient whenever $k_S$ is zero or one. For $k_S\ge2$, choose two distinct surviving tours with that pattern. Their pair-union generator specializes **exactly to $Z_S$** in $\mathbb F_2[Z]$, since all their guarded arcs are present and their unguarded arcs coincide. Either circuit answer therefore gives the correct residue modulo $D_y$. This holds coefficient by coefficient, so $P(y,Z)-h_n(y,Z)\in D_y$ on every fiber. The isomorphism proves the claimed membership in $D+B_A$.

For a single vertex, leaving all its outgoing arcs unrestricted gives $n-1$ patterns. Leaving both its incoming and outgoing arcs unrestricted gives $(n-1)(n-2)$ patterns: the predecessor and successor are distinct for $n\ge3$. The latter choice leaves $2(n-1)$ arcs outside $A$ and touches every input row and column. Thus a polynomial-size promise circuit would allow these exceptions even though the congruence treats them as formal variables.

## Finite verification

Run `python3 small_cover.py`. On four vertices the checker enumerates all six rooted tours and all $3^{12}=531{,}441$ choices of a formal arc set and a Boolean assignment to its complement. It compares the tour count of each forced-outgoing restriction with direct grouping by pattern. For all 5,525 fiber-pattern pairs where the promise permits a coefficient different from parity, it checks divisibility by a specialized pair-union generator in the ordinary monomial ideal. It also checks the vertex-cover pattern bounds on all $2^{12}$ formal arc sets and the exact pattern counts for the two stars. [Reproduction script](small_cover.py)
