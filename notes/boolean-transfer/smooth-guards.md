# Nonlinear Boolean identities and the rank of their derivatives

Work in the ordinary polynomial ring over $\mathbb F_2$ on the $M=N(N-1)$ loopless arcs. Let $G=(G_1,\ldots,G_r)$ be polynomials computed by a joint polynomial-size arithmetic circuit. Allow the identities $G_i^2=G_i$ and all errors in the two-tour ideal $D_N$. Let $P$ be a polynomial-size circuit output congruent to $h_N$ under these equations. The following necessary conditions extend the affine case to specified nonlinear systems.

* If the Jacobian of $G$ on the Boolean router baselines has the same row space of dimension $r$, then $M-r\le N\exp(O((\log N)^{3/4}))$. In particular this holds for $G_i=L_i+H_i^2$ with independent linear parts $L_i$ and polynomial-size guards. The $H_i$ may be nonlinear and may share variables.
* For completely general $G$, full row rank at one explicit Boolean baseline already gives $r=\Omega(N)$. The argument below fixes the baseline before inspecting $G$. A hypothesis of full row rank means that the $r$ rows at that point are independent. Redundant nonlinear equations need separate justification before being dropped.

The circuits for the guards matter because the proof must compute corrections to a formal substitution. Arbitrary unrestricted Boolean circuits still have all $M$ coordinate identities available. [Affine case and routing baselines](affine-compression.md)

## Freezing guard values through a finite order

Fix a Boolean baseline $c$, and write $J$ for the $r\times M$ matrix of first derivatives of $G$ there. Suppose $J$ has full row rank. Choose a right inverse $T$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Use an independent formal parameter $t$ and coefficients that are polynomials in target variables $Y$. Begin with any first-order vector $z_1(Y)$ whose coefficients lie in $\ker J$, and suppose $z_1,\ldots,z_{j-1}$ already freeze every guard through order $j-1$. Form
$$
 R_j=[t^j]\left(G\left(c+\sum_{a<j,\ a\ge1} t^a z_a\right)-G(c)\right),
 \qquad z_j=T R_j .
$$
Replacing the argument by its previous value plus $t^j z_j$ adds $t^j Jz_j$ modulo $t^{j+1}$. This follows by expanding monomials: a term with one new factor uses the baseline in all other factors at order $j$, and a term with two new factors has higher order. Since $JT$ is the identity, the residual cancels in characteristic two. Iterating for $2\le j\le m$ yields
$$
         G\left(c+\sum_{a=1}^m t^a z_a\right)=G(c)\pmod{t^{m+1}}.
$$
Each $G_i(c)$ is a Boolean scalar, so the substituted $G_i^2-G_i$ and all polynomial multiples vanish through order $m$. Any polynomial in these $G_i$ also agrees with its baseline value through that order. The same holds for the Boolean identity of such a polynomial.

To implement the construction, propagate coefficient lists to order $j$ at the gates of the joint guard circuit for each $j$. Addition is componentwise and multiplication uses truncated convolution. The previously computed $z_a$ are shared wires. Dense multiplication by the fixed right inverse takes $O(Mr)$ gates per order, and an $s_G$-gate guard circuit costs $O(m^3(s_G+M+1))$ gates over all orders. This is polynomial in $N,m,s_G$. Only finite polynomials are needed. [Lean lemma on scalar agreement modulo an ideal](AffineGuard.lean) · [Executable lift and singular example](smooth_guards.py)

## Recovering the tour polynomial

For the first assertion take the common kernel $K$ of the Jacobian rows at the router baselines. The grouping lemma supplies disjoint outgoing sets and singleton incoming ports with independent crossing sums on $K$. This determines the router and its Boolean baseline $c$. The actual Jacobian there has full row rank and kernel $K$. Choose $z_1(Y)$ so that the crossing sums are $Y_{ij}$ off the diagonal and zero on the diagonal. Apply the finite lift just proved.

In any spanning tour at least one arc must exit each outgoing ring, and at least one must enter each incoming port. All those arcs have baseline zero. At order $t^m$ the same $m$ arcs must serve both roles, each using exactly its first-order term. All other arcs use the baseline. The original routing proof therefore gives $[t^m]h_N=h_m(Y)$ under the substitution. Two distinct tours cannot use the very same $m$ crossing arcs and otherwise only baseline arcs. Every generator of $D_N$ vanishes through order $m$. Thus $[t^m]P=h_m(Y)$, and truncated circuit evaluation contradicts the pinned bound whenever the grouping lemma supplies too large an $m$. This proves the first assertion with the same exponent as for affine forms. More generally, one may use the intersection of all the Jacobian kernels in the grouping lemma, provided the Jacobian at the selected baseline has full row rank. ## A fixed baseline for arbitrary smooth guards

Take $m=\lfloor N/3\rfloor$, one outgoing vertex and one incoming vertex per city, and one anchor per city, padding one anchor with the remaining vertices. Fix baseline one on the anchor-to-outgoing and incoming-to-anchor arcs and on the padding path. All other arcs have baseline zero. This baseline is independent of the guards. Suppose their Jacobian has full row rank $r$ here. Project its kernel to the $m(m-1)$ off-diagonal arcs from outgoing to incoming vertices. The image $S$ is a linear subspace of codimension $u\le r$, since taking a coordinate image cannot increase codimension. The diagonal crossings can be ignored because a self-loop cannot occur in a spanning cycle of $m\ge2$ cities.

If $r\le m/4$, even these constrained first-order weights allow a large formal tour polynomial. Give $S$ a full-row-rank defining matrix with $u$ rows and choose $u$ pivot arc columns. List their distinct tails as $a_1,\ldots,a_p$, with $p\le u$. Select distinct filler vertices $f_1,\ldots,f_p$ outside **all endpoints** of all pivot arcs. At most $2u$ endpoints are excluded and $m\ge3u$. The remaining $m-2p$ vertices, after removing the tails and fillers, form a complete active core. If $p=0$ use the ordinary $h_m$ unchanged. Otherwise choose a core vertex $o$. Keep core arcs weighted by their own variables, but replace the outgoing interface of $o$ by the forced path $o\to a_1\to f_1\to\cdots\to a_p\to f_p$, with its arcs set to one. The original arcs out of $o$ now leave $f_p$ instead. All other nonpivot arcs are zero. Solve the $u$ equations of $S$ for the pivot entries as affine polynomials in the core variables, constants included. Each $f_i$ still has just one possible incoming arc $a_i\to f_i$, since no pivot ends at a filler. Hence every surviving tour must take that outgoing choice at $a_i$, and no pivot arc can occur. Contracting the path gives exactly $h_{m-2p}$.

Lift these affine crossing weights in $S$ to some $z_1(Y)$ with coefficients in $\ker J$ using a linear section of the coordinate image. The independent parameter $t$ places the constant parts of these weights at first order too. Apply the same finite lifting and tour counting at the fixed baseline. Now $[t^m]P=h_{m-2p}(Y)$, with $m-2p\ge m/2$. For polynomial-size guards and $P$ this contradicts the pinned formal lower bound for all large $N$. Thus the hypothesis of full row rank at this fixed baseline rules out $r\le\lfloor N/3\rfloor/4$ for polynomial-size families. [Pinned circuit lower bound](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L33-L46)

Full row rank is used to solve for each higher-order correction, not merely to choose the first-order directions. For $G(x)=x^2$ at zero, the Jacobian vanishes, so $x=t$ is an allowed first-order tangent. Every correction of order at least two leaves the $t^2$ coefficient of $G(x)$ equal to one. The program reproduces that failure, checks lifts at all 32 baselines of a nonlinear test system and compares truncated composition with independent full expansions. It also enumerated 20,448 candidate host tours for 60 affine systems on five to seven cities. All 420 surviving tours avoided their pivot arcs, and each inner projection equalled the required formal tour polynomial. The lifting and circuit bounds above are written proofs.
