# From formal Hamiltonian-cycle lower bounds to Boolean decision circuits

**An equivalent Boolean target.** It is enough to rule out polynomial-size circuits that answer correctly on every graph having zero or one directed tour, even when their answers on all other graphs are arbitrary. Witness isolation and a solution-preserving graph reduction give the converse simulation. Over the field of two elements, parity of the number of tours is a valid promise answer whose canonical polynomial has no positive-surplus layers at all. The unrestricted size lower bound remains open. The formal size bound survives arbitrary errors supported on unions of two tours, even when Boolean identities on a limited set of arc bits are allowed. A polynomial-time greedy test agrees with existence on a large explicit family of dense unique-tour graphs and already fails on a cycle with one extra chord. [Equivalence, proof and scope](unique-promise.md) · [Exact polynomial quotient](unique-promise-ideal.md) · [Dense unique-tour intervals](unique-tour-windows.md)

**Attempts to get an unrestricted Boolean size lower bound.** Over the field of two elements, an arithmetic computation can call a cube-correct Hamiltonicity circuit many times on inputs computed from earlier calls. For a representative obtained by repeated squaring, every call can be replaced by a scalar through the target degree. Recovering the hard tour polynomial by this interface already costs superpolynomially many ordinary gates. Inspecting the Boolean gates gives a different necessary condition. Under a small set of edge labels that rules out spurious low-degree parses, almost every tour in a polynomial-size circuit requires many repeated positive edge labels in every accepting parse. Showing that small circuits must violate this tradeoff remains open. [Adaptive oracle theorem](oracle-transfer.md) · [Guarded Boolean parses](guarded-parses.md)

**Affine computations.** In an XOR–AND decision circuit for Hamiltonicity, the input parity vectors reaching AND operands span every arc coordinate. More generally, covering accepting assignments by affine spaces requires many spaces or a large number of partial parity assignments along an input ordering. These are restrictions on Boolean computations. [Rank and affine-cover bounds](affine-transcripts.md)

**A restriction on Boolean circuits themselves.** Every circuit deciding directed Hamiltonicity on $N$ vertices has treewidth $\Omega(N)$ in the undirected wire graph with one shared input vertex per arc bit. A bag-by-bag calculation reconstructs the canonical polynomial under a width bound. Using nonnegative weights exposes the formal tour polynomial as the lowest-degree part, and a known compilation theorem removes the dependence on Boolean circuit size. This is a structural lower bound. The deterministic logspace exact-tour language also has this width requirement. [Proof, model and sources](decision-treewidth.md)

**A logspace language with hard dense layers.** For the exact-tour test, $k+1$ zero-or-one substitutions into its degree-$N+k$ polynomial recover a smaller formal tour polynomial after a signed sum. For every fixed $\alpha<1/2$ and every surplus sequence $0\le k(N)\le\alpha N^2$ eventually, these individual layers are VNP-complete under polynomial-size oracle reductions over every field. Hence hard layers over this entire range are compatible with polynomial-size Boolean decision circuits. [Construction and integer identity](easy-dense-layers.md)

**Hardness of individual layers.** For each fixed $k\ge3$, the degree-$N+k$ part of the canonical polynomial for directed Hamiltonian-cycle existence is VNP-complete over every field. Swapping the incoming arcs of two roots joins an arbitrary graph to a bidirected $k$-cycle. The two orientations cancel after a signed substitution into the layer, leaving exactly the formal tour polynomial on $N-k$ vertices. The same formal layers occur in a deterministic logspace comparison language. A size bound for computing the canonical layer from a Boolean circuit remains open. [Projection, VNP membership and checks](layer-projection.md)

**Layers at larger surplus.** Dense graphs whose arcs form one orbit under a prime-power group extend the formal arithmetic lower bound to every $3\le k=o(N^2)$ in each fixed characteristic. In characteristic two the substitution uses only zero and one and spends at most $8\sqrt{k}$ vertices. Characteristic zero uses one additional homogeneous-component extraction. [Proof and verification](orbit-padding.md)

**Which coefficients survive.** Over the field of two elements every nonzero positive-surplus coefficient comes from a unique partition into forced paths. Counting the contracted graphs gives an exact formula in $N$ for every fixed surplus. The labelled core counts through surplus six and the complete truth tables through five vertices are reproducible. [Classification, counts and examples](anf-cores.md)

**Follow-up result.** If a polynomial-size arithmetic circuit over the field of two elements decides directed Hamiltonian-cycle existence on every $m$-vertex graph, one monomial in its formal output must involve at most $m$ distinct edges and repeat at least $\Omega((\log m)^{1/3})$ of them. Earlier, we bounded only the union of the repeated edges across many monomials. The compiler sums the same partial interpolations on the original DAG, allowing the repeated edges to vary between monomials. [Proof and resource bounds](sparse-repeats.md) · [Lean theorem](SparseRepeat.lean) · [Executable compiler](sparse_repeats.py)

**Limit of this approach.** Testing whether the selected edges *are exactly* one spanning tour is in deterministic logspace. Its degree-$m$ canonical component is the very same formal tour polynomial, and Lean proves that the same correction recovers it on this easier language. Thus the repeat lower bound also occurs in a problem with a polynomial-time algorithm. A separate OR restriction, using published saturated graphs, gives a linear lower bound for unrestricted AND gates with free XOR. [Easy comparison language](EasyTourLayer.lean) · [Degree and multiplication bounds](degree-literature.md)

**How far the agreement extends.** For any fixed $C$, an explicit deterministic logspace language matches every canonical Hamiltonicity layer through $m+C\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor$. For any fixed $\delta>0$, a polynomial-time language matching all layers through $m+\lfloor m^\delta\rfloor$ for every sufficiently large $m$ exists if and only if P = NP. The proof uses a sparse-graph algorithm in the first regime and a forced-path padding reduction in the second. Intermediate widths also have a conditional classification, given below. [Proof, algorithms and verification boundary](degree-prefix.md)

**Log-squared and larger prefixes.** Matching through $m+\lfloor(\log_2 m)^2\rfloor$ in deterministic polynomial time is equivalent to a $2^{O(\sqrt{n+e})}$-time Hamiltonicity algorithm on graphs with $n$ vertices and $e$ arcs. More generally, positive-rate ETH rules out a uniform polynomial-time match at every width $m+k(m)$ with $k(m)/\log m\to\infty$. These are conditional conclusions. The reduction contracts forced arcs while preserving tours and their edge weights. [Equivalence and kernel](surplus-kernel.md) · [3-SAT implication and sources](prefix-eth-sources.md)

**The first nonzero degree of directed Hamiltonian-cycle existence recovers the formal Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial.** Let a bit indicate each possible directed edge on $m$ labelled vertices. Over the field of two elements, let $F_m$ be the unique multilinear polynomial for “the input graph contains a Hamiltonian cycle.” Its degree-$m$ homogeneous component is exactly the polynomial bounded by the source theorem. This gives a restricted arithmetic-circuit lower bound for an NP **decision** problem and a precise obstruction to extending it. [Proof and executable construction](boolean_jet.md) · [Source theorem](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L34-L46)

For an edge set $T$, its multilinear coefficient is the XOR of the decision values on all subsets of $T$. When $T$ has $m$ edges, every proper subset is too small to contain a tour. The coefficient is therefore one exactly when $T$ itself is a tour. For $m\ge3$, existence and parity agree through degree $m+2$. The first corrections occur at degree $m+3$, precisely on unions of two tours differing on three outgoing arcs. For three vertices, let $u$ and $v$ be the degree-three monomials of the two opposite tours. Parity is $u+v$ and existence is $u+v+uv$. On the complete loopless digraph both tours are present, so the degree-six correction changes the answer from zero to one. [Lean proof of the degree-$m$ identity](TourLayer.lean) · [Proof of the first correction](boolean_jet.md#first-disagreement-between-existence-and-parity-of-tours)

## What this implies for circuit size

Suppose an unrestricted-degree arithmetic DAG of size $S$ computes the right decision bit on every graph. Inspect its **formal output after cancellations**. Let $B$ contain all edge variables that occur with exponent at least two in any surviving monomial involving at most $m$ distinct variables, and put $b=|B|$. For all sufficiently large $m$, the source's positive constant $c$ gives

$$
1+(m+1)^2\big[2^b(S+b+1)+2b\big]
  > m^{c(\log_2 m)^{1/3}}.
$$

To see why, specialize just the $b$ variables in all possible ways and recombine the resulting circuits with Boolean selectors. This reduces their positive exponents to one. Monomials involving more than $m$ variables still have degree greater than $m$, and every remaining low-support monomial is now multilinear. Extracting degree $m$ by gate-by-gate convolution therefore computes the **formal** tour polynomial. The displayed left side counts every copied circuit, selector and convolution gate, including input and constant gates. The construction needs only a supplied $B$ and allows arbitrary sharing. The algebraic identity is checked as `partial_component_of_correct`. [Lean theorem](TourLayer.lean) · [Construction, exact bound and proof](boolean_jet.md#formal-output-tradeoff-for-an-np-decision-problem)

With polynomial-size circuits, this forces $b\ge c(\log_2 m)^{4/3}-O(\log m)$. In particular, polynomial size is impossible for multilinear outputs or for outputs with only $O(\log m)$ such coordinates. A checkable syntactic sufficient restriction counts the distinct variables shared by the two children of any multiplication. The unrestricted problem permits many repeated variables, even when the Boolean circuit is small. A polynomial-size Boolean decision family, if one exists, would therefore give an explicit family whose canonical multilinear representatives require superpolynomial formal circuits. [Syntactic version and resource bounds](balanced.md#a-restricted-transfer-with-a-constructive-gate-bound)

## What the upstream incidence gap requires

The order-three projective-plane task L-meta-1494 asks for a 64-gate lower bound on a partial Boolean function. The counting argument from this companion proves 64 whenever each incident pair has an accepting parse with just its two input leaves. This includes syntactically multilinear monotone circuits of arbitrary depth. A separate Lean proof gives 38 for all permitted circuits. Unrestricted monotone circuits may multiply signals depending on the same input. The exact finite search has 52 required inputs and 560 maximal forbidden inputs. A bounded solver run left the unrestricted lower bound open. [Proof and scope](arc-window.md) · [Finite search](arc_window_search.py)

## Where the source proof needs more

* **Balanced decomposition, E.4.** The source takes the coefficient of one degree-one variable from each block. If a product gate contributes a formal squarefree monomial, its children partition the variables in that monomial. After Boolean multilinear reduction the children can overlap. For example, the reduced product of $x_1 x_2$ and $x_1$ has full coefficient one, although the disjoint-split formula gives zero. This breaks the gate identity used by this proof. The existential possibility of a different decomposition remains open. [Source map](source-map.md#two-source-operations) · [Gate identity and replacement](balanced.md)
* **Low-degree evaluation, K.2 and K.3.** K.2 correctly evaluates an ordinary formal truncation at an encoded tree, including for a polynomial of high degree. K.3 uses a degree hypothesis to identify this with the full value. The canonical Boolean low-support truncation is function-invariant and admits direct computation on the Hamming ball with one evaluation per ball point. The ball can be exponential when the cutoff grows. At the next Hamming layer the black-box method already needs every proper-subset value in the worst case. A weighted program with the paper's run bounds does not follow from this construction. [Source map](source-map.md#exact-use-in-k3) · [Boolean replacement](boolean_jet.md#what-survives-of-the-papers-evaluation-lemma)

Over the field of two elements the converse size simulation also holds on the cube. Each arithmetic addition is XOR, expressible as $(a\lor b)\land\neg(a\land b)$ in four Boolean gates. Each multiplication is AND and each field constant is one bit. Keeping earlier wires gives at most four Boolean gates per arithmetic slot. Thus excluding every cube-correct arithmetic family is itself a general Boolean circuit lower bound. This converse is a written observation.

The circuit families in the conditional theorem are nonuniform, indexed by the number of input bits, and the constants and exponent in a polynomial bound must work at every length. Directed tour existence is in NP by its vertex-order certificate. The Lean theorem has $m^2$ arc variables. For $m\ge2$ a tour uses no diagonal arc, so ignoring those coordinates gives the loopless encoding with $m(m-1)$ bits. One can reject other string lengths or use an explicit size header. For SAT the length parameter would be the full encoded formula length. A deterministic polynomial-time machine can be unrolled into polynomial-size Boolean circuits, hardwiring each length and simulating its configurations for the time bound. Thus a lower bound of the stated unrestricted kind for a language in NP would imply P ≠ NP. A nonuniform upper bound alone would provide no uniform algorithm. The argument uses neither an exact-counting algorithm nor a decision-to-parity reduction. [Conditional theorem](CircuitTransfer.lean) · [Background](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pnp.pdf)

This companion uses the arithmetic theorem at a pinned revision and leaves its source and targets intact. The earlier affine-elimination work concerns projecting exact relations. In particular, testing whether selected edges **are** a tour and testing whether a graph **contains** a tour are different decision functions. This companion uses the latter throughout the lower-bound argument. [Earlier research](https://github.com/Ma1achy/pequalnp-proofs/tree/30709949f9116bd1200223a54751e0d600e6d4e0/notes/affine-elimination/cycle2)

The companion is on a research branch of the P versus NP contributor fork. The project's desk accepts a single Lean proof of an assigned statement. This work supplies checkable lemmas and a target for future Boolean circuit gaps. [Contribution rules](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)
