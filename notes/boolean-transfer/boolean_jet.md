# Boolean-invariant low-degree extraction

**Main restricted-model conclusion.** Let a size-S arithmetic DAG over F₂ agree on every loopless m-vertex directed graph with the NP decision predicate “some directed Hamiltonian cycle exists.” Let b be the number of distinct edge variables that occur with exponent at least two in a nonzero output monomial whose support contains at most m distinct edges. For all sufficiently large m, the formal Hamiltonian-cycle lower bound from the source gives an absolute c>0 with

    1 + (m+1)² [2ᵇ(S+b+1) + 2b] > m^{c (log₂ m)^{1/3}}.

In particular S polynomial in the graph encoding length requires b≥c(log₂ m)^{4/3}−O(log m). This allows arbitrary internal sharing, powers and cancellation. It applies to formally multilinear output after cancellation as the special case b=0, irrespective of the formal degrees of the intermediate gates. The property of the final polynomial is a restriction; the proof does not supply it for unrestricted Boolean circuits. [Source theorem and HC monomials, Statement.lean lines 34–46](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L34-L46)

## The Boolean low-support ring

For N bits, identify a point with its support U⊆[N]. Over F₂, the multilinear representative of a function f has monomial coefficients

    a_T = XOR_{U⊆T} f(U),       f(U) = XOR_{T⊆U} a_T.

Substitution proves the inverse formulas: a strict subinterval contributes 2ʲ copies for j>0, hence zero in F₂. These coefficients prove existence and uniqueness without any assumption about finding a small circuit for the representative. In the Boolean quotient xᵢ²=xᵢ, so the squarefree monomials e_A multiply by e_A e_B=e_{A∪B}. The span of all e_A with |A|>d is an ideal. The **Boolean jet** J_d(f) is the class modulo that ideal, represented by the coefficients a_T for |T|≤d, or equivalently by the function values f(U) on the Hamming ball |U|≤d. Its kernel consists exactly of the functions zero on that ball. Evaluation on the ball is a ring isomorphism from the truncated quotient to a product of B=Σ_{i≤min(d,N)} C(N,i) copies of F₂.

Addition of jets is XOR. Multiplication in coefficient coordinates sums a_A b_B over all A∪B=T. Multiplication in evaluation coordinates is pointwise AND. Representing the result by its degree-≤d ANF and evaluating that representative on an arbitrary dense point is a linear operation; it does not make evaluation on a point outside the ball a ring homomorphism. For example with d=1, J₁(x₁x₂)=0 although evaluating the two retained linear factors at (1,1) gives 1.

**Algorithm and costs.** For each ball point, run the given S-node DAG in topological order, retaining previously computed gate bits. Arbitrarily many uses of one gate read the same bit and every addition includes cancellation over F₂. Sort the B support masks, then form the restricted subset zeta transform by processing dimensions i one at a time and XORing the cell for T without i into the cell for T whenever i∈T. Every predecessor stays inside the ball. The Boolean operations are O(SB + dB). The implementation obtains predecessor indices by binary search on sorted N-bit masks and records at most dB index pairs. For explicit bit costs take L=2+N+ceil(log₂(S+B+2)). Allow O(L²) bit operations for a mask operation, an addressed lookup or a loop counter update (addresses have O(L) bits), and allow O(L) comparisons per binary search. An upper bound for the complete implementation is O((S+N+d+1)B L³) bit operations and O((S+(N+d+1)B)L²) bits of space, including the input, masks, sorting by comparison, the reusable per-point gate array and transform. Truncate d to N first if supplied larger. Coefficients have one bit. This gives a polynomial bound for fixed d in an explicit-variable input; with very large binary variable identifiers one can renumber occurring variables first. Variable d can make even the explicit output exponential.

**Counterexample to a coefficient-count measure.** The function δ₀=∏ᵢ(1+xᵢ) has every squarefree coefficient equal to one. A DAG of at most 3N+1 nodes attains all B nonzero jet coefficients simultaneously. Thus counting nonzero coefficients of an explicit jet cannot distinguish the target from all small circuits by an upper-versus-lower size gap. The dimension B is also the exact amount of information in an unrestricted explicit ball truth table: every ball assignment extends to a Boolean function.

## What survives of the paper's evaluation lemma

The paper's Lemma K.2 concerns ordinary homogeneous components of a *formal* polynomial and a particular program model. In the pinned Lean source `line_coeff_eq` identifies a line-substitution coefficient with evaluation of a homogeneous component; `Paper.lem_lowdeg` produces the sum of components of ordinary degree ≤d at one-hot codewords. The statement bounds trie size, trie height, program size and the cost of the specified runs, and evaluates their aggregate value. The paper's mapping clarifies which run costs Lean bounds. The degree recurrence splits a positive degree into smaller degrees at internal products; chain constants absorb zero-degree factors. See §2.5, Appendix K.2, and the formalization mapping in the paper. [Paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/paper.pdf) · [LowDeg.lean lines 19–52](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Paper/LowDeg.lean#L19-L52) · [LowDegChains.lean lines 19–27](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Paper/LowDegChains.lean#L19-L27)

The Boolean jet does descend to agreement on all Boolean inputs. Its multiplication permits A=B=T. Consequently the positive-degree split from the proof fails verbatim even for g=x·x: the constant terms vanish and no two positive degrees sum to one, yet the degree-one ANF part of g is x. Repeated squaring gives a DAG with s squarings, formal monomial degree 2ˢ, Boolean support one and an unfolded multiplication tree with 2ˢ leaves. This refutes a leaf bound by support size for that unfolding. It does not preclude a different fast program for the represented function.

There is a precise oracle barrier to one method for dense queries. For a visible support W of size t>d, the value of the truncated multilinear representative is

    XOR_{U⊆W, |U|≤d} [C(t−|U|−1, d−|U|) is odd] f(U).

Expand the inverse formulas and use Σ_{j=0}^a C(b,j)=C(b−1,a) mod 2 for a<b, by Pascal's rule. At t=d+1 every term has coefficient one, requiring exactly 2ᵗ−1 deterministic black-box value queries in the worst case. On the all-zero transcript, any omitted proper U⊊W can be singled out by the point indicator on W, a product of t literals with O(t) gates; it agrees with every answer unless that query matched U on W. Queries outside W cannot distinguish more than one such pattern per query. For t≤d the value is simply f(W), one query. This lower bound concerns exact deterministic oracle queries. It does not lower-bound the source's sum over runs with preprocessed constants or white-box circuit algorithms.

On a paper codeword, the active support has one coordinate per present letter and one for the end marker if it fits, hence size at most the number n of places. Thus J_n(f) evaluated there always gives the full Boolean answer. An explicit B-entry algorithm does not supply the source's fast run bound and constant budget when d=n grows. The proof of K.3 uses d=n on its degree-bounded Φ with the required codeword values. The fields and conclusions of K.3 and the HC version have additional hypotheses described in the source. [Code definition, Nc/Acc/Prog.lean lines 159–161](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Nc/Acc/Prog.lean#L159-L161) · [Paper, Appendix K.2, pp. 56–57](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/paper.pdf)

**Restricted success in that program model.** Suppose the formal output g itself has degree at most e in each variable, with arbitrary DAG reuse internally. On a codeword of support t, each surviving monomial of g has total degree at most et. Apply the *original* K.2 to g with cutoff en: at every such codeword its returned sum equals the full g-value. Its published constants/trie bounds remain valid, with the program-size and time parameters evaluated at d=en. A sufficient weaker hypothesis is that every nonzero monomial whose support is contained in a legal codeword has degree at most the chosen cutoff. This is a promised condition on the output; no recognition algorithm or general degree control is asserted. Outputs that are formally multilinear after cancellation permit e=1. A syntactic sufficient condition is disjoint variable sets at every multiplication gate. This observation addresses the evaluation step only and supplies no Boolean replacement for the balanced decomposition step.

## First disagreement between existence and parity of tours

Use a loopless complete directed graph on m≥3 labelled vertices with independent bits for its arcs. A tour is a successor permutation consisting of one cycle. Counting by permutations counts each cycle once. HC over F₂ evaluates to parity. Its formal polynomial is squarefree homogeneous of degree m, with one monomial for each tour. The paper includes diagonal variables; for m≥3 none occurs in a tour monomial. A circuit in the loopless variables therefore computes the same formal polynomial after the natural inclusion in the paper's variable set. [Source definition](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L34-L36)

Two distinct tour permutations disagree on at least three outgoing edges. They cannot disagree on just one because they are bijections. If they disagreed on two, their relative permutation would be a transposition, but both m-cycles have sign (−1)^{m−1}. Thus a graph with at most m+2 edges has at most one tour. The Boolean ANF jet of tour existence and that of parity coincide through degree m+2. In particular the ordinary degree-m homogeneous component of the unique multilinear representative of tour existence is **the prescribed formal HC polynomial**.

The threshold is sharp, and one can describe the entire next layer. Choose any tour and cut three arcs; the remaining edges make three paths, allowing paths consisting of a single vertex. A tour reconnection using all different outgoing arcs at the cuts must match the three tails to the three heads by the other cyclic order of the three paths. Indeed there are exactly two cyclic orders of three labelled paths; one is the original, the other changes all three connecting arcs. The union of both tours has m+3 edges. Its only possible successor permutations are those two. One way to see this is to view their outgoing-to-incoming matchings: the three distinct pairs of changed edges form one alternating cycle of length six, with exactly two perfect matchings. The remaining arcs are forced. Conversely any graph on m+3 arcs with two tours is exactly a union of this form. On such a set existence gives one and parity gives zero. Every proper subset has agreement, so the ANF coefficient of their difference at that set is one by the subset inversion formula. Every other set of the same cardinality contributes zero. Each union is counted from its two tours, giving (m−1)! C(m,3)/2 monomials in the first correcting layer. This is an NP decision statement for the graph predicate, without an assumption that Boolean decision computes arbitrary counts.

## Formal-output tradeoff for an NP decision problem

Let g be the formal polynomial computed by any cube-correct S-gate DAG over F₂ for the directed tour existence problem. Let B contain the variables occurring with exponent ≥2 in any nonzero coefficient monomial of g of support size ≤m, and write b=|B|. No restrictions are placed on repeated variables in larger-support monomials. Define partial multilinearization by

    P_B(g) = Σ_{a∈{0,1}^B} g(a,x_outside) · ∏_{i∈B:a_i=1} x_i · ∏_{i∈B:a_i=0}(1+x_i).

By linearity it suffices to apply this formula to one monomial. Every positive exponent of a B variable is replaced by one because its value on 0 and 1 agrees with the variable; a coordinate missing from the monomial stays absent. Exponents of all other variables stay unchanged. Thus monomial supports are preserved until coefficient cancellation. With the stated B, every support-≤m monomial is fully multilinearized. Every larger-support monomial still has total degree >m. Therefore, as a formal identity,

    homogeneous_m(P_B(g)) = homogeneous_m(multilinear representative of g) = HC_m.

This is a nonuniform circuit construction, and an explicit compiler if B is supplied. Use one copy of the entire original DAG per assignment a, replacing its B-input leaves by constants, and compute each selector product with O(b+1) gates. Add all products. For b≥1, preallocate b variables, their b complements and a single one in 2b+1 gates. Each of 2ᵇ copies uses at most S gates, including its possibly newly constant input leaves, and at most b multiplications by the selected literals. Adding the terms uses 2ᵇ−1 further gates. Thus 2ᵇ(S+b+1)+2b gates suffice. For b=0, use the original DAG directly. For any circuit for P_B(g), compute ordinary homogeneous parts 0 through m at all gates. Constants and variables have their usual components; addition is componentwise; at a product the component j is Σ_{i=0}^j g_i h_{j−i}. For a product, j+1 multiplications and j additions for coefficient j sum to exactly (m+1)² gates across j=0,…,m. An addition uses at most m+1 gates and each variable or constant uses one, with a single shared zero for absent parts. Hence the displayed outer factor and the additive one suffice, with explicit reuse of earlier components. Formal degree can be arbitrarily high; in the ordinary grading higher components cannot return to degree m. Taking component m computes HC_m with the bound at the top of this note. For an effective construction with supplied B, polynomial bit overhead per emitted gate suffices to write all binary references: their lengths are O(b+log(S+b+m+2)), and the simple copy, selector and convolution loop counters have at most this many bits.

Apply the source's main theorem in characteristic two for all sufficiently large m. If S≤m^a for a fixed a, then b≤m(m−1) and taking log₂ gives b≥c(log₂ m)^{4/3}−O_a(log m). Equivalently, permitting only O(log m) such repeated-variable coordinates forces S to be superpolynomial. The input length for the graph problem is m(m−1) adjacency bits together with a size header, so this is a restricted lower bound in the explicit input length. Guessing a permutation and checking its m edges takes polynomial time. The proof uses the arithmetic theorem on the **derived formal HC circuit**. It does not claim that every small Boolean decision circuit produces a representative satisfying the restriction, or that one can efficiently discover B or the formal support from an arbitrary circuit.

The Lean theorem `BooleanTransfer.TourLayer.partial_component_of_correct` gives the displayed polynomial identity with the actual upstream `Statement.hc` over the field of two elements. It quantifies over all polynomials with the stated coverage and decision hypotheses. The circuit construction realizing this partial reduction at the displayed cost and the asymptotic deduction are written proofs. ## Verification

Run `python3 boolean_jet.py` in this directory. It checked 262,948 products in low-support quotients and 8,402 coefficient/evaluation identities by exhaustive three-variable-or-smaller enumeration across all cutoffs. It checked 864 DAG/cutoff pairs with repeated wires and seed 314159, 448 random partial-reduction identities with seed 271828, the dense δ₀ family through eight variables, and thirty self-squarings. For m=3,4 it checked all 64 and 4,096 loopless directed graphs respectively and transformed both truth tables to ANF. The first correction has 1 and 12 monomials. Through m=7 it also verified the number of distinct three-switch unions (1,12,120,1,200,12,600 in order), checking the exact two-tour count per union through m=5. The finite checks exercise the claims. Mathlib's formal homogeneous component identity for partial versus full exponent clipping is kernel-checked over any commutative semiring in `PartialLayer.lean`. The same module proves that partial clipping preserves cube evaluations and that the full clipping is exactly the unique multilinear representative modulo the Boolean ideal for finite variable sets over an integral domain. Its pinned Lean 4.33.1 check reported only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The circuit gate bound, its asymptotic consequence, and the first correcting layer are mathematical proofs.

The ordinary arithmetic DAG compiler `compile_layer(gates, chosen, degree)` shares both selector literals for each chosen variable across all copies, sums the copies with one fewer addition than terms, and uses at most j additions for each degree-j product component. It skips operations with the shared zero when possible. On a 15-gate three-vertex decision circuit containing a fourth power of one edge it emits **24 gates** against the finite upper bound of 577. Direct expansion over F₂, keeping formal exponents, finds exactly the two tour monomials. Taking the original formal degree-three part without partial reduction retained only one. Another 640 formal-expansion and finite-bound checks cover constants, cancellation, degree zero, all eight subsets of three coordinates, and sixteen seeded circuits (seed 173205).
