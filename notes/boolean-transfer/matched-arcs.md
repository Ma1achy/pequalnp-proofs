# How many Boolean arc identities can a small circuit leave out?

Write $h_N$ for the ordinary directed Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial on $N$ labelled vertices. It has one squarefree monomial per spanning tour. In the polynomial ring over $\mathbb F_2$ on the $M=N(N-1)$ loopless arc variables, let $D_N$ be the ideal generated by $X_{T\cup U}$ for distinct tours $T,U$. For a set of arcs $A$ let $B_A$ be generated by $X_e^2-X_e$ for $e\in A$.

**Coordinate bound.** Suppose a size-$N^{O(1)}$ circuit outputs a formal polynomial $P_N$ with $P_N-h_N\in D_N+B_A$. Then every arc outside $A$ touches a set of at most $\exp(O((\log N)^{3/4}))$ vertices. In particular at most $N\exp(O((\log N)^{3/4}))=N^{1+o(1)}$ arcs can be left outside $A$. This strengthens the earlier bound on the cardinality of $A$. The open case allows all $M$ Boolean identities. [Exact promise quotient](unique-promise-ideal.md)

**Change of basis.** Over $\mathbb F_2$ one may instead grant the identities $L(X)^2=L(X)$ for an arbitrary family of affine linear forms. If the linear parts have rank $r$, a polynomial-size solution still requires $M-r\le N\exp(O((\log N)^{3/4}))$. Thus for any fixed $\delta>0$, leaving $N^{1+\delta}$ independent directions unconstrained already forces superpolynomial size. This assertion concerns the rank of the equations permitted for the output congruence. It does not count gates that explicitly square a wire. ## Putting the formal variables on a matching

For $m\ge3$ write $q=m(m-1)$. Make an anchor $c_i$ and two directed rings of $m-1$ ports per city $i$. The outgoing ring $O_i$ has a port $o_{ij}$ for each $j\ne i$. The incoming ring $I_i$ has a port $p_{ji}$ for each $j\ne i$. Join $c_i$ to every port in $O_i$ and every port in $I_i$ to $c_i$. Give these arcs and all ring arcs weight one. Give the arc $o_{ij}\to p_{ij}$, which enters $I_j$, the variable $Y_{ij}$. Give every remaining arc weight zero. The $q$ variable arcs have $2q$ distinct endpoints. All port weights come from $\{0,1\}$ or are distinct individual variables.

The template has $2q+m$ vertices. To use more, split one anchor into a path through the additional vertices, with every original incoming edge pointing to the first vertex and every outgoing edge leaving the last. Every spanning tour using only nonzero arcs must follow the path. Given any prescribed matching of $q$ directed arcs on the host, relabel the ports so that the variable arcs occupy that matching, using other vertices for the anchors.

A spanning tour can enter $O_i$ only from its anchor, so it visits the entire ring consecutively. The chosen last port uniquely determines the first one. Likewise all of $I_i$ must be traversed consecutively, since its only exit goes to its anchor. Its chosen first port determines the traversal. Contracting the cities yields a single spanning directed cycle on $m$ cities. Conversely every such cycle lifts uniquely with exactly its own variables as weights. These are identities of formal polynomials with integer coefficients.

Let $\pi$ be this substitution. Then $\pi(h_N)=h_m$. Moreover $\pi(D_N)\subseteq D_m$. A pair-union generator involving a tour with a zero-weight arc maps to zero. Two surviving distinct tours contract to distinct tours, and their squarefree union maps to the squarefree union of the two contracted tours. Using each variable on a single arc preserves its exponent on a shared edge. If the prescribed variable arcs avoid $A$, then $\pi(B_A)=0$. Weighted graph substitutions are classical in algebraic complexity. Valiant's reduction for the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial uses weighted directed graphs. Single-visit port gadgets with specified entrance and exit conditions also occur in directed Hamiltonicity reductions. Here the two rings permit every choice of predecessor and successor and retain the squarefree pair-union error ideal. [Valiant, Theorem 3](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/colombia/references/valiant.completeness.1979.pdf#page=9) · [Glynn, Haythorpe and Moeini, Definition 1.1 and §5](https://researchnow-admin.flinders.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/15745229/Glynn_Directed_P2018.pdf#page=2)

## From a matching to the size bound

Take a maximum vertex-disjoint matching of $k$ directed arcs outside $A$. Put $m=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor$ for sufficiently large $k$. Then $q\le k$ and $2q+m\le2k\le N$. Project a circuit of size $s$ by the construction above. Its output differs from $h_m$ only in $D_m$. No generator of that ideal has degree at most $m$, so the lowest nonzero component is $h_m$. Over the Laurent series field, substitute $\epsilon Y$ and scale the output by $\epsilon^{-m}$. The resulting approximate circuit for $h_m$ uses at most $2s+3$ slots when inputs are counted. The pinned approximate lower bound gives
$$
                 m^{c(\log_2 m)^{1/3}} < 2s+3
$$
for an absolute $c>0$ and sufficiently large $m$. In particular, polynomial $s$ forces $k\le\exp(O((\log N)^{3/4}))$. The $2k$ endpoints of a maximal matching cover all the remaining arcs. Each vertex has $2(N-1)$ incident directed arcs, giving the stated bound on their total number. The constants in the upper bounds can depend on the polynomial size exponent. [Pinned approximate lower bound](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L48-L61)

## How close this comes to the Boolean question

There is a conditional converse. If a small zero-versus-one-tour Boolean circuit exists, identities can be left out on **all** arcs incident with any fixed number of vertices. For one vertex, this leaves $2(N-1)$ arc variables formally unrestricted. The proof groups tours by their predecessors and successors at those vertices. Pairs of tours with the same surviving pattern account for every possible coefficient error. Thus a linear number of exceptions is compatible with the small-circuit hypothesis. Whether a small circuit exists with all identities available remains equivalent to the unrestricted Boolean question. For arbitrary Boolean-correct formal representatives, applying the sparse-repeat argument after every relabelling of this template also forces many different patterns of jointly repeated, vertex-disjoint arcs in the formal output. A polynomial-size product can itself contain many such patterns. [Counting proof and limitation](repeated-matchings.md)

The graph bijection and asymptotic bounds above are written proofs. Lean checks the algebraic transport given the routing hypotheses. Independent enumeration tested the formal projection across eight relabelled and padded hosts of up to 48 vertices and checked the unions of all 584 pairs of surviving tours. The general change-of-basis argument uses the grouping lemma, an expansion by homogeneous degree, and a separate Lean lemma on the order of projected products.
