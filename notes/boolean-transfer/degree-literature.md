# Near-full degree for directed Hamiltonicity

Let $H_m$ be the Boolean decision function for a directed Hamiltonian cycle on $m$ labelled vertices. Its input has $N=m(m-1)$ independent bits, one for each ordered pair of distinct vertices. If a matrix encoding includes the diagonal, fix those $m$ bits to zero. For every $m\ge19$, put $q=N-2\lceil3m/2\rceil$. Then

$$
\deg(\operatorname{ML}_{F}(H_m))\ge q
\quad\text{over every field }F,
\qquad
\operatorname{MC}_{\mathbb F_2}(H_m)\ge q-1.
$$

Here $\operatorname{ML}_F$ is the unique multilinear polynomial agreeing with the decision function on the Boolean cube. Over $\mathbb F_2$ this is its algebraic normal form. Multiplicative complexity counts binary AND gates in a Boolean DAG with free XOR, constants and fan-out. Equivalently it counts multiplication gates in a division-free $\mathbb F_2$ arithmetic circuit required to give the correct Boolean answers. The following is a written corollary using a published graph-existence theorem.

**Restriction to OR.** Lin, Jiang, Zhang and Yang establish the existence of a maximally non-Hamiltonian undirected graph $G$ on $m$ vertices with $\lceil3m/2\rceil$ edges for all even $m\ge20$ and all odd $m\ge17$. These ranges cover every $m\ge19$. Such a graph has no Hamiltonian cycle, and adding any missing edge creates one. Replace each edge of $G$ with both arc orientations. The resulting digraph $D$ has no Hamiltonian cycle because forgetting its directions would give one in $G$. For any missing arc $u\to v$, adding the undirected edge $uv$ gives a Hamiltonian cycle that must use $uv$. Remove that edge and orient the remaining spanning path from $v$ to $u$ in $D$. Adding the single arc $u\to v$ now closes a directed Hamiltonian cycle. Lichiardopol and Zamfirescu explicitly give this directed construction in the paragraph after Theorem 2.5. [Lin et al., *Ars Combinatoria* 45 (1997), pp. 263–270, abstract on p. 263](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/ars/Volume%20045/volume-45-paper-21.pdf#page=1) · [Lichiardopol and Zamfirescu, *Ars Mathematica Contemporanea* 16 (2019), p. 63](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/8588703/8588705#page=5)

Fix the arcs of $D$ to one. Exactly $q$ arc bits remain free. When they are all zero the decision value is zero, and setting any one of them to one gives a tour which persists under further additions. Thus the restricted function is $\mathrm{OR}_q$. Over any field its multilinear polynomial is $1-\prod_{i=1}^q(1-x_i)$, whose degree-$q$ coefficient is $(-1)^{q+1}\ne0$. Fixing coordinates of a multilinear polynomial preserves multilinearity and cannot raise its degree. This proves the degree bound. It also bounds the total degree of every polynomial representing $H_m$ on the cube, since replacing each positive variable exponent by one cannot increase degree.

**From degree to multiplication gates.** Consider any circuit over $\mathbb F_2$ with $t$ multiplications, numbered in topological order. Temporarily regard their outputs as independent bits $y_1,\dots,y_t$. Following just additions back from either input to multiplication $i$ expresses that input as an affine form in $x,y_1,\dots,y_{i-1}$. Write $y_i=A_iB_i$ for these two forms. The circuit output is affine in $x,y_1,\dots,y_t$. Starting there, substitute $A_iB_i$ for $y_i$ in descending order of $i$, multilinearizing in all remaining variables after each substitution. Every stage preserves values on consistent bit assignments. Each current monomial either omits $y_i$ or contains it once. Substitution replaces that one factor by a polynomial of degree at most two, increasing total degree by at most one. Multilinear reduction and cancellations can only decrease degree. After $t$ steps the result is the unique multilinear representative and has degree at most $1+t$. Applying this to $H_m$ gives $t\ge q-1$, with arbitrary sharing allowed. This is the classical Boolean multiplicative-complexity degree bound. [C. P. Schnorr, “The Multiplicative Complexity of Boolean Functions,” AAECC 1988, LNCS 357 (1989), pp. 45–58](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-51083-4_47) · [Meltem Sönmez Turan, statement in §1 and reference 8](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=935024)

The degree lower bound is $N-O(\sqrt N)$, within an additive $O(\sqrt N)$ of the maximum possible degree. The multiplication lower bound is also linear in the encoding length. The OR restriction itself can be computed with $q-1$ multiplications, so the restriction alone cannot rule out polynomial-size circuits for directed Hamiltonicity.
